# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a chat, use abekia if the point is "apa kabar", then put the errand in Indonesian. For a public counter, the pemda names are Anaileuita or Maronsita, still with the errand in Indonesian. Thanks, if a Mentawai word is wanted, is surak or sura', with the spelling note.

## Sample Tasks

- Open a Sipora office note with Maronsita, then the missing-document line in Indonesian.
- Ask a friend "abekia" and keep the rest in Indonesian.
- Review a text that treats Siberut and Pagai as the same spelling.
- Explain why anai leuk ita is glossed ucapan selamat, not automatically "apa kabar".
- Refuse a healing or wedding formula.

## Register And Semantics

- Ask the island before a printed line.
- Keep the Indonesian meaning before any Mentawai word is added.
- Skip jokes about Mentawai communities, and skip invented adat lines.
- Leave medical, legal, and money terms in Indonesian.
- ale as "hai" is for men addressing men in the dictionary. Do not aim it at everyone.
- Code-mixing is the safe shape. Do not complete it into a fake full translation.

## Review Checklist

- Does the output use Indonesian grammar with one Mentawai word and call the whole line Mentawai?
- Is every Mentawai word in the local mirror or in a user sample?
- Is the island named, or explicitly unspecified?
- Are abekia, anai leuk ita, and the pemda sapaan kept distinct?
- Is a healing, wedding, or uma ceremony request left unwritten?
- Are names, dates, and facts unchanged?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first, then the island note.
- Review request: name the risk, then a safer line.
- Public text: one named sapaan plus Indonesian facts, and a speaker-review note.
