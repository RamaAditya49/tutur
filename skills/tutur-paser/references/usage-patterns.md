# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

Return the message first. A Paser phrase from the kamus stands when its gloss is the point: a time-of-day greeting, a how-are-you, or permission toward an older person. The appointment stays Indonesian. Say mixed when both languages appear. Say that the kamus line is not tagged to one of the ten dialects.

## Sample Tasks

- Chat: `selamat ma` or `ise kabar iko?`, then Indonesian for the plan.
- Public reminder: Indonesian for the hour and the place. A greeting may open it if the time of day is known.
- Admin: `man taka ngadap Bupati` only when the speech act is "let's go face the bupati". A form deadline stays Indonesian.
- Adat: name soyong and stop. Do not write the request.
- Review: flag Kutai `mendi`, `nyawa`, `etam`, and any sentence that pretends to be Long Kali without a Long Kali source.

## Review Checklist

- Is this Paser, not Kutai or Banjar?
- Does every Paser span have a kamus gloss?
- Is the ten-dialect gap stated when the village was not confirmed?
- Is `iko` still you, and `ise` still the question word from the entry?
- Are soyong and burial steps absent?
- Are dates and places still Indonesian?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?
- If the user named Bajau Pondong or Kutai, was the text refused as the wrong language?
