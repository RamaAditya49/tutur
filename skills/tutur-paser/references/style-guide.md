# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Paser, Peta Bahasa name Bahasa Pasir (Paser).
- Region: Kabupaten Paser and Penajam Paser Utara, Kalimantan Timur.
- Glottocode: `pasi1257`
- ISO 639-3: not assigned.
- Not Kutai. Not Bajau Pondong, which Peta Bahasa maps as a different language inside parts of Paser and Berau.

## Safety

- Do not invent vocabulary or soyong.
- Do not assign `aji` or `andén`. The kamus records them as titles, not as words to hand out.
- Do not treat the seventeen Hasan names and the ten Peta Bahasa dialects as the same list.
- Do not copy Kutai phonology. Paser examples keep `iko`, `ise`, `depo`, `tabe`.

## Everyday

Greet with the time word: `ma`, `nayap`, `malom` after `selamat`. Ask `ise kabar iko?`. Toward an older person, `tabe depo ulun tuo`. Then Indonesian for the errand. Code-mixing is the normal way to finish.

## Public

A hall notice needs a clock and a place in Indonesian. `selamat ma` can open a morning call. It does not carry the schedule. Do not pick Swan Slutung wording for a Long Kali notice without a local sample.

## Professional

`man taka ngadap Bupati` is the spoken "ayo menghadap Bupati" from the kamus. Letters, forms, and deadlines stay Indonesian. Do not expand that one clause into a full official letter.

## Adat Boundary

The kamus preface names mulung and soyong for requests around forest and field, and it describes older burial practice. Those steps and any soyong text stay with a mulung. This skill has no soyong line to repeat, so it does not write one. `nang derhaka depo ulun tuo` is a prohibition in the dictionary, not a ceremony script.
