# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Simalungun. Glottolog name: Batak Simalungun. The dictionary headword `sahap` means kata, and `marsahap` means berkata. This mirror does not invent a sentence `sahap Simalungun`.
- ISO 639-3: `bts`. Glottocode: `bata1288`.
- Peta Bahasa: dialek Simalungun of Bahasa Batak. Map line: Kabupaten Simalungun and Kabupaten Tanjung Balai. Difference from dialek Toba: 69,25 percent.
- Dictionary: Kamus Bahasa Simalungun–Indonesia, edisi kedua, Balai Bahasa Provinsi Sumatera Utara. PDF: https://repositori.kemendikdasmen.go.id/18777/1/KAMUS-SIMALUNGUN-INDONESIA.pdf
- Source confidence: Level 2.
- Abbreviation list in that kamus: Ry Raya, Sp Sipispis, Tb Toba. The PDF's front matter does not add a village map for Raya and Sipispis. Do not invent one.

Source mirror notes:

- Simalungun is not Toba, even though both maps sit inside a Batak heading and this dictionary uses `horas`.
- Mauliate was searched in the PDF and not found.
- Karo `mejuah-juah` and Pakpak `njuah-njuah` are different openers. Do not put them here.
- `amang` is glossed ayah. It is a noun in the pronunciation examples, not a title for every man in the room.

## Local Usability

Use this mirror for a chat, a short reminder, or a review. One printed sentence, then Indonesian.

Two words for you are both real and not the same line. `ho` [hO] is kau; anda, with `laho huja ho?` (mau ke mana kau). `ham` is kamu; kau; anda, with `laho hu ja do ham in?` (kamu mau ke mana). Keep the spelling of the example you quote.

`ahu` is the pronoun headword, aku, in `ahu mamasa buku` (saya membaca buku). The salam example uses `au`: `jabat lobei au`. Do not normalize one into the other.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Short notes from the dictionary. Not a copy of the page.

- `horas`: selamat; ucapan selamat dan berbahagia yang biasa dipakai orang Batak saat bertemu. Example: `haganup hanami makkatahon horas` (kami semua mengucapkan selamat). `hanami` in that entry is kami.
- `ulang ho hanam i jin`: jangan kamu marah di situ. Headword `hanam`, marah.
- `sahap`: kata. `ia marsahap hubakku`: ia berbicara kepada saya.
- `domma sahei haganupan`: semuanya sudah selesai. Headword `sahei`, selesai.
- `rumah ai handalan tumang`: rumah itu bagus sekali. Headword `handalan`, bagus.
- `laho huja ho?`: mau ke mana kau. Headword `ho`.
- `laho hu ja do ham in?`: kamu mau ke mana. Headword `ham`.
- `ahu mamasa buku`: saya membaca buku. Headword `ahu`, aku.
- `jabat lobei au, tongkin nari misirma ho`: salam dulu aku, sebentar lagi engkau akan pergi.
- `andigan do ahu marhasil`: kapankah aku akan berhasil. Headword area `andigan`, kapan.
- `eta`: ayo. `olo`: ya. From the lafal examples, not from a longer sentence.
- `amang`: ayah. Do not use it as a general pak.
- `jabu`: rumah, tagged Tb. Do not offer it as unmarked Simalungun.
- Written e is generally pronounced /E/. Written o is generally /O/. Example shapes in the lafal note: `oak` suara, `boli` beli, `oto` bodoh.

## Generation Rules

- `horas` may open a meeting. Do not add mauliate after it.
- Do not paste Toba kinship (tulang, hula-hula, boru, ito, lae) into this language.
- Do not replace `rumah` in `rumah ai handalan tumang` with `jabu`.
- Do not merge `ho` and `ham`, or `ahu` and `au`.
- `eta` and `olo` stay single words. Do not build `eta laho huja ho` unless the user supplied that sentence.
- Label mixed text as mixed.
- Aksara and adat verse stay with a speaker. The kamus mentions surat sapuluh siah and this skill does not write characters.
