# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Simalungun. ISO 639-3 `bts`, glottocode `bata1288`.
- Not Batak Toba, not Karo, not Pakpak.
- Peta Bahasa calls it a dialect of Bahasa Batak and prints 69,25 percent difference from dialek Toba. The dictionary is still its own book.
- `sahap` means kata. Do not invent a language motto around that gloss.
- Phrases in this skill: examples from Kamus Bahasa Simalungun–Indonesia, edisi kedua.

## Safety Rules

- Do not invent vocabulary.
- Do not import mauliate or the Toba kinship set.
- Do not ignore a Tb tag.
- Keep medical, legal, school, and government details in Indonesian.
- A full sentence that is not in the source stays untranslated.
- Written e and o follow the kamus note: e is generally taling, o is generally /O/. Do not respell the quoted line.

## Registers

### Everyday

`Horas` opens a meeting. A cousin on the road gets `laho huja ho?` and then Indonesian for the actual plan. `Olo` is the short ya from the lafal note. `Ulang ho hanam i jin` is a real scolding, not a template for every refusal.

### Public

A reminder can open with `horas` or, if the group is wishing people well, `haganup hanami makkatahon horas`. The day, the place, and the instruction stay Indonesian. Do not add a kinship roll call.

### Professional

Admin, school, and clinic messages stay in Indonesian. `Horas` at the top is enough if the office wants a local opening. Do not translate the form, the fee, or the deadline. `Ahu mamasa buku` is a dictionary example about reading a book, not a school announcement.

### Adat

Do not write umpasa, a ceremony speech, or aksara Simalungun. The kamus mentions surat sapuluh siah as the name of the letters. Ask a speaker who writes that script. `Amang` means ayah in the kamus. It is not a title to put on every guest.
