# Offline Brief

## Local Facts

- Purpose: ordinary messages, reminders, and review for Bahasa Simalungun.
- Catalog: ISO 639-3 `bts`, glottocode `bata1288`. Glottolog name: Batak Simalungun.
- Peta Bahasa files it as dialek Simalungun of Bahasa Batak, in Kabupaten Simalungun and Kabupaten Tanjung Balai, 69,25 percent from dialek Toba.
- This skill treats it as its own language. It does not generate Toba, Karo, or Pakpak sentences.
- Dictionary labels inside the kamus: Ry Raya, Sp Sipispis, Tb Toba, Kr Karo, Mly Melayu. A Tb tag means the lemma is marked Toba.
- Pronunciation note in the kamus: written e is generally /E/ (taling). Written o is generally /O/.
- Particles in that same note: `eta` ayo, `olo` ya.
- Phrase source: Kamus Bahasa Simalungun–Indonesia, edisi kedua. Level 2.
- `horas` is in this dictionary. `mauliate` was not found in the file that was read.
- This file is the offline substitute. It does not replace the dictionary.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent vocabulary, kinship grids, or adat verse.
- Do not add Mauliate, Tulang, or hata Batak.
- `horas` is allowed here only as the dictionary's selamat. Do not paste the rest of a Toba greeting around it.
- Do not use a lemma tagged Tb. `jabu` is tagged Tb for rumah.
- `ahu` and `au` are not a free choice. Quote the sentence that contains each one.
- `ho` and `ham` are separate you-pronouns with their own examples. Do not swap them.
- Keep schedules, money, health advice, and official instructions in Indonesian.
- If a full sentence is not in the dictionary, write a short attested phrase and leave the rest in Indonesian. Label that text mixed.

## Safe Output Patterns

- Open with `horas` when a meeting greeting is wanted.
- For a group wishing well, the printed line is `haganup hanami makkatahon horas`.
- `eta` is ayo and `olo` is ya, as short words, not as a new sentence frame.
- For an unknown word, keep the Indonesian word.
- For adat or aksara, ask for the wording the family uses.
