# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a family chat or a guest note in Baubau, return the message first. Open with `tabea`. Put the errand in Indonesian. Mark the line mixed. Do not dress the message up as Keraton Wolio.

## Sample Tasks

- Rewrite a short Indonesian message with a Wolio greeting.
- Review a text for Muna substitution or consonant-final "Wolio".
- Ask where someone is going with the attested `yapai ta porope ingkita?` when the addressee is respected.
- Write a neighborhood reminder that uses `inda pokia` only in the sense tidak mengapa.
- Keep a homestay note's room number and price in Indonesian.
- Refuse to compose sara or sultanate lines, and say what would be needed instead: a source sentence from the community.

## Register And Semantics

- `ingkita` is the respectful you in the dictionary. `ingkoo` is engkau. Do not swap them for tone decoration.
- `tabea` covers salam, permisi, and maaf. Pick the sense that matches the message.
- `tulungi` means tolong. It is not a greeting.
- Keraton Baadia is a dialect place. Do not imitate it.
- Prices, permits, and medical instructions stay in Indonesian.

## Review Checklist

- Does every Wolio word end in a vowel?
- Is `tabea` used as salam, permisi, or maaf, matching the dictionary?
- Is `ingkita` reserved for the respectful you or for kita, not used as a random filler?
- Did any Muna, Cia-Cia, or Tolaki form slip in?
- Is a keraton or sara request left uncomposed?
- Are numbers, dates, and places unchanged?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?
- Did the answer avoid copying a dictionary page?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first.
- Review request: list the non-Wolio or keraton risk, then a shorter everyday version.
- Public text: one Wolio opener, Indonesian instructions.
