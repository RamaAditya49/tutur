# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

Return the message first. If a Maanyan line is included, gloss it in the note, not by inventing a second Maanyan sentence. Hours, money, addresses, and form deadlines stay Indonesian. Say mixed when both languages are in the message.

A cousin chat can open with `amahku tulak bagawi` or `aku naan hengaw lawah` and then the plan in Indonesian. A village note can open with `takam ganyah ngakali ngampi luput urusan yiti` only when the point is that the group is still sorting the problem.

## Sample Tasks

- Chat: one glossed line, then Indonesian for the time and place.
- Public reminder: Indonesian for the schedule. Add a Maanyan line only if the gloss matches the announcement.
- Admin: keep the deadline in Indonesian. `kami penduduk tumpuk yiti` can open a village note; it does not replace the form instructions.
- Adat: name the register (taliwakas, hiyang wadian, kalakar) and stop.
- Review: flag Ngaju or Banjar vocabulary, unglossed Maanyan, and any new pangunraun sentence.

## Review Checklist

- Is the language Maanyan, not Ngaju, Banjar, Bakumpai, or Lawangan?
- Does every Maanyan span appear with its gloss on the teaching page or in Linarto?
- Is the place named, or is the unlabeled teaching page admitted?
- Is pangunraun left unextended?
- Are dates, money, and places still Indonesian?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?
