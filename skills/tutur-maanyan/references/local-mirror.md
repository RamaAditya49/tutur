# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language/register: everyday Bahasa Maanyan. Not pangunraun.
- Region/context: Barito (Kalimantan Tengah) and Warukin (Tabalong, Kalimantan Selatan).
- Glottolog: Ma'anyan, glottocode `maan1238`.
- ISO 639-3: `mhy`.
- Peta Bahasa idb=95. Two dialects inside Kalimantan Tengah, Batapah and Malungai. Warukin is the same language at subdialect distance.
- Dictionary layer: Balai Bahasa Kalimantan Selatan, Kamus Dwibahasa Maanyan-Indonesia (1.158 lema and 805 sublema announced on the 2025 page) and Balai Bahasa Kalimantan Tengah, Kamus dwibahasa Dayak Maanyan-Indonesia, 2018. Entries were not copied.
- Everyday sample: teaching page, 22 January 2012, numbered lines with Indonesian glosses.
- Ritual description: Linarto on Pangunraun Taliwakas Paadu, data from Jaweten, Dusun Timur, Barito Timur.

## Local Usability

Use this offline. Repeat a glossed teaching-page line when its meaning is the point. Do not treat that page as Batapah, Malungai, or Warukin specifically; it does not name a village.

Linarto's etymology note, cited from Santoso, Ngabut, and Erlan: mak 'menuju' and anyan 'tanah datar atau tempat datar'. That is a published account of the name, not a greeting.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Only lines whose Indonesian sits on the same teaching-page item:

- `ulun yiti hengaw anniku` — orang ini teman adikku.
- `aku naan hengaw lawah` — saya ada teman lama.
- `amahku tulak bagawi` — ayahku pergi bekerja.
- `lewu yeru haut matueh` — rumah itu sudah tua.
- `kami batelu bagawi` — kita bertiga bekerja.
- `takam ganyah ngakali ngampi luput urusan yiti` — kita sedang berusaha menyelesaikan masalah ini.
- `ulun-ulun yeru manre` — orang-orang itu tidur.
- `ranu hungey yeru tanang` — air sungai itu tenang.
- `hanye midi sandal ma anakni` — dia membeli sandal untuk anaknya.
- `aku basarita` — saya bercerita.

Pieces that show up inside those glossed lines: `ulun` orang, `yiti` ini, `yeru` itu, `hanye` dia, `here` mereka (in `here ipamupuk`, mereka saling memukul), `aku` saya, `hang` in `hang wading` dibalik, `haut` sudah, `naan` ada, `tumpuk` kampung in `kami penduduk tumpuk yiti`. `takam` and `kami` are both glossed with kami/kita on that page. Do not invent an inclusive/exclusive rule from that.

Linarto, everyday versus pangunraun, not for reuse as ceremony: everyday `wanawang` 'pintu' against the pangunraun line `tukat amai wanawang suluk talaga rampan`; everyday `tumpuk` 'kampung' against `tumpuk natat bantai rawi ruak tumpungan`. Quote only to show the gap. Do not continue the pangunraun line.

## Generation Rules

1. Ask which Maanyan place if the user did not name one.
2. Put the practical point in Indonesian.
3. Add one glossed Maanyan line only when the gloss is the thing being said.
4. Keep Ngaju and Banjar out. If a Warukin speaker supplied Banjar, label it Banjar.
5. Do not write taliwakas, hiyang wadian, or a mantir monologue.
6. Do not mark the result as official or native-verified.
