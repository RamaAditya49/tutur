# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

A usable Tidung-aware message is short. Put one attested Sesayap phrase up front when it matches the situation, then say the practical part in Indonesian. If nothing in the phrase list fits, stay in Indonesian and say so.

## Sample Tasks

- Reply to a cousin who asks whether a shirt or a pair of trousers fits.
- Tell a neighbor your stomach hurts and you will go to the clinic later.
- Write a kampung reminder about river water. Keep the time and the place in Indonesian.
- Review a text that calls itself Bahasa Tidung but is Banjar or Kutai wording.
- Separate a Sesayap request from a Berusu or Tagul request.

## Review Checklist

- Is every Tidung word on the Salimbatu list, or in a sample the user supplied?
- Is the dialect named: Sesayap, Berusu/Belusu, or Tagul?
- Did the output recombine words into a sentence the source does not contain?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?
- Are Kutai and Banjar particles absent?
- Are Northern Tidung (`ntd`) forms absent?
- Are names, dates, prices, and instructions unchanged?
- Is adat language absent unless the user pasted it?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first, dialect note second.
- Review request: name the unsupported words, then a shorter rewrite.
- Public request: Indonesian carries the instruction. Tidung stays a labeled greeting or complaint.
