# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Tidung. Catalog name for the Kalimantan Utara coastal entry: Southern Tidung, ISO 639-3 `itd`, glottocode `sout3241`.
- Not Kutai. Not Banjar. Not Northern Tidung (`ntd`, glottocode `nort3262`).
- Dialects on the Badan Bahasa map: Berusu/Belusu, Sesayap, Tagul.
- Phrases in this skill: dialek Sesayap, recorded in Desa Salimbatu. Juata Laut in Tarakan Utara is listed in the same dialect group, but these sentences were collected in Salimbatu.

## Safety Rules

- Do not invent vocabulary.
- Do not invent a possessive or a plural from `-ku`.
- Do not paste Banjar or Kutai politeness into a Tidung line.
- Keep medical, legal, school, and government details in Indonesian.
- Ask which village when the text will be printed or read aloud.
- A full sentence that is not in the source stays untranslated.

## Registers

### Everyday

Use a Salimbatu phrase the way a cousin would: a complaint, a size, a person in the family. Then Indonesian.

Attested openings include `tinaiku keliwot duwol` and `kenjulu tupar seluarku`.

### Public

A reminder can quote `timuk desungai noh risih dan saluy` only as the water description from that study. The date, the meeting place, and what people should do stay in Indonesian. Name the dialect.

### Professional

Admin, school, and clinic messages stay in Indonesian. A single labeled phrase is enough if the office wants a local opening. Do not translate the form, the fee, or the deadline.

### Adat

Do not write ritual Tidung. News about the Nunukan dictionary says younger speakers often cannot follow adat wording. That is a reason to ask a practitioner, not a reason to compose a formula.
