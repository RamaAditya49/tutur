# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Tidung. Other catalog names include Tidong. ISO 639-3 `tid` was retired on 2016-01-15 and split into Southern Tidung `itd` and Northern Tidung `ntd`.
- Glottocode for the Kalimantan Utara coastal catalog entry: `sout3241` (Southern Tidung). Family glottocode: `tidu1239`. Northern Tidung: `nort3262`.
- Badan Bahasa map: one language, three dialects (Berusu/Belusu, Sesayap, Tagul), spoken in Bulungan, Malinau, Nunukan, and Tarakan.
- Phrase variety in this file: dialek Sesayap as recorded in Desa Salimbatu, Kecamatan Tanjung Palas Tengah, Kabupaten Bulungan. Peta Bahasa also lists Kelurahan Juata Laut, Tarakan Utara, under dialek Sesayap. The sentences were not collected in Juata Laut.
- Source confidence: Level 1.
- Links: see `sources.md`. Do not browse for new words at generation time if this file does not already list them.

Source mirror notes:

- Tidung on this map is not Kutai and not Banjar.
- Peta Bahasa gives about 90–92 percent difference between Tidung and Long Pulung, Lundayeh, and Tenggalan.
- Balai Bahasa Kalimantan Timur collected adat, clothing, house, festival, food, flora, and fauna vocabulary in Tarakan in February 2022. That list is not reproduced here.
- The 1981 grammar and the 2022 Nunukan dictionary are real publications. Their headwords are not in this mirror.

## Local Usability

Use the phrases for a cousin chat, a short health complaint, or a neighbor note when the rest of the message can stay in Indonesian. A village announcement, a school worksheet, or an adat speech needs a speaker of the named dialect.

Code-mixing is already in the published examples (`seluar`, `tapi`, `dan`). Mixed output should be labeled mixed.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Full phrases from the Salimbatu study, with the gloss printed beside them:

- `kenjulu tupar seluarku` — terlalu besar celanaku.
- `tupar kenjulu seluarku` — besar terlalu celanaku. The discussion also glosses the same idea as celanaku sangat besar.
- `seluarku tupar` — celanaku besar.
- `duwol keliwot tinaiku` — sakit betul perutku. The table also glosses this line as perutku sakit sekali.
- `tinaiku keliwot duwol` — perutku terlalu sakit.
- `kenjulu duwol tinaiku` — terlalu sakit perutku.
- `timuk desungai noh risih dan saluy` — air di sungai itu bersih dan dingin. The discussion also says jernih for `risih`. Keep both glosses visible. Do not normalize the spelling to a form the paper does not print on the example line.
- `yandu ulun noh jajok tapi marot` — istrinya cantik, cuman jahat.
- `pelita baloi noh banta` — lampu rumahnya terang.
- `inan tiriku` — mama tiriku.
- `pensulotku` — saudaraku.
- `bais nyawo` — baik hati, inside the longer line about Siska.

Words the paper's discussion actually defines:

- `kenjulu` — penguat derajat, terlalu.
- `tupar` — besar.
- `seluar` — celana. `seluarku` — celanaku.
- `duwol` — sakit.
- `tinai` — perut. `tinaiku` — perutku.
- `timuk` — air.
- `risih` — bersih pada tabel; jernih pada pembahasan.
- `saluy` — dingin.
- `yandu` — istri.
- `ulun` — orang.
- `jajok` — cantik.
- `marot` — jahat.
- `baloi` — rumah.
- `banta` — terang.
- `bais` — baik.
- `pintor` — pintar, in the line about `pensulotku`.

`noh` appears inside several of those phrases. The paper does not give it one stable one-word gloss, so quote the whole phrase instead of defining `noh` alone.

## Generation Rules

1. Write the meaning in Indonesian first.
2. Add a Tidung phrase only from the list above, or from a sample the user just provided.
3. Keep the phrase intact. Do not recombine `tupar` with a new noun.
4. Say dialek Sesayap, Salimbatu, when the phrase is one of these.
5. Leave Berusu and Tagul requests in Indonesian until a sample from that dialect is supplied.
6. Label mixed text as mixed.
7. Do not produce a mantra, a wedding formula, or a government translation.

## Register Handling

- Everyday: one Salimbatu phrase plus Indonesian.
- Public: Indonesian for the instruction, with the Tidung phrase labeled as a Sesayap sample.
- Professional: Indonesian for dates, places, and duties.
- Adat: ask for the family's wording. Do not compose it.
