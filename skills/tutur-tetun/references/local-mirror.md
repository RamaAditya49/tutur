# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Tetun of Belu and Malaka, inside the broader Tetum languoid.
- Glottocode: `tetu1245`. ISO 639-3: `tet`.
- Family in Glottolog: Austronesian, Malayo-Polynesian, Timor-Babar, Eastern Timor, Central Timoric A, Tetunic, Tetum.
- Peta Bahasa treats the Lakekun isolek as its own language against Bunak and Dawan. It does not describe Tetun Dili.
- Williams-van Klinken separates Tetun Terik (Fehan, Foho, and eastern south-coast Tetun) from Tetun Dili. Speakers of one often find the other hard to follow. Tetun Dili has lost much of the Terik subject marking and has many Portuguese loans.
- Fehan subject prefixes on h-initial verbs, from the Fehan sketch: 1sg `k-`, 2sg `m-`, 3sg `n-`, 3pl `r-` or `n-`. This skill does not build new prefixed verbs from that rule.
- Uma Lor transcripts use a practical spelling without the apostrophes of the Fehan sketch.
- The 2019 Badan Bahasa textbook states that its Tetun is one of the two official languages of Timor-Leste. Its greeting `Diak ka lae?` belongs to that book, not to the Uma Lor sample.

Source links live in `sources.md`. This file does not copy the dictionary or the grammar.

## Local Usability

Use this file offline to decide what can be said in Tetun and what stays in Indonesian. Ordinary family messages can open with an attested word. Public notices, school text, and adat speech need a speaker from the named community.

Fehan and Foho are both Bahasa Tetun in Indonesia, and they are not the same wording. Ask which community if the user names only "Tetun".

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Only items checked against the sources below.

- `He'e`: ya, or sudah. Women and men. Everyday and adat, in the Uma Lor study. Adnyana 2018.
- `Hi'i`: ya. Women, informal only. Adnyana 2018.
- `Bai hau ba kare tia batar lai`: kakek, saya pergi lihat jagung dulu. Adnyana 2018. The free translation in the article also mentions the kitchen; the gloss line does not.
- `Yani nabe?`: Yani di mana? Adnyana 2018.
- `Mai Bapa ita lao.`: ayo Bapak, kita berangkat. Adnyana 2018.
- `Awan Bapa.`: besok, Bapak. Adnyana 2018.
- `Bolu!`: panggil. Adnyana 2018.
- `Haa baa!`: makan saja, tanpa saya. Fehan sketch.
- `Mai ita la'o liu dei.`: mari kita jalan terus saja. Fehan sketch.
- Pronouns in the Fehan sketch: `ha'u` saya, `oo` kamu, `ita` kamu hormat dan kita (termasuk lawan bicara), `nia` dia, `ami` kami, `emi` kalian, `sia` / `sira` mereka.
- Family words in the Uma Lor study: `ina` ibu, `ama` ayah, `mau` kakak laki-laki, `bi` kakak perempuan. A mother may call a girl `bete` or `bu'i` and a boy `mane` or `bo'o`. A father uses the personal name.
- `makoan`: juru bicara adat in the Fehan community description. Not a word to build new ritual speech from.

## Generation Rules

1. Build the meaning in plain Indonesian first.
2. If the user wants Belu or Malaka, take the opening from the Uma Lor or Fehan list above.
3. Keep one spelling. Uma Lor: `hau`, `lao`, `nabe`. Fehan sketch: `ha'u`, `la'o`.
4. Do not conjugate a new verb with `k-`, `m-`, or `n-` unless that exact word is in a source or a user sample.
5. Leave names, dates, money, and places as the user wrote them.
6. Label mixed text as mixed.
7. If the user asks for Tetun Dili, switch sources and say so. Do not hide a Dili line inside a Belu message.

## Register Handling

- Everyday: `He'e`, a name plus `nabe`, or `Mai Bapa ita lao`, then Indonesian for the detail.
- Public: one attested invitation at most. The notice itself stays in Indonesian.
- Professional: operational sentences stay in Indonesian.
- Adat: do not write a new makoan turn. The recorded adat lines in Adnyana 2018 are evidence of what was said in one event, not a script to reuse.
