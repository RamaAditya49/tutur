# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name in this skill: Bahasa Tetun of Belu and Malaka.
- Also called, in the sources: Tetun Fehan, Tetun Foho, Tetun Terik. These are local varieties, not synonyms of Tetun Dili.
- Codes for the Glottolog languoid: ISO 639-3 `tet`, glottocode `tetu1245`.
- Not covered: Tetun Dili / Tetun Prasa / official Timor-Leste Tetum, Bahasa Dawan, Melayu Kupang.
- Speakers code-mix with Indonesian. That mix is the honest register for anything longer than a sourced line.

## Safety

- Do not invent vocabulary, ritual language, titles, or cultural formulas.
- Do not present the 2019 textbook greeting as Belu usage.
- Do not assign `bete`, `bu'i`, `mane`, or `bo'o` to officials or guests.
- Do not build new subject-marked verbs from the Fehan prefix list.
- Ask which community when the user says only "Tetun".

## Everyday

Household chat can open with `He'e` or with a sourced line such as `Yani nabe?` or `Bai hau ba kare tia batar lai`. The next sentence, if it is not in the source, is Indonesian. A woman's informal agreement may be `Hi'i`.

## Public

A community reminder may use `Mai Bapa ita lao` only as the attested invitation to set off, and only when the addressee fits `Bapa`. Clock times, venues, and instructions stay in Indonesian. Do not expand this into a slogan.

## Professional

Office, school, clinic, and government wording stays in Indonesian. A Tetun word is limited to an attested acknowledgement such as `He'e` when the user is answering a family message, not when the user is writing a letter.

## Adat Boundary

`makoan` is the recorded name for an adat spokesperson in the Fehan description. This skill does not write marriage, death, or house-ritual turns. Lines recorded inside an adat drinking conversation stay inside that citation. They are not templates.
