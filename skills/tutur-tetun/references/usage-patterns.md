# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a text to family or neighbours, give the message first. One attested Tetun phrase, then Indonesian. Say which variety the phrase comes from when the user might expect Tetun Dili.

## Sample Tasks

- Rewrite a short Indonesian chat for a cousin in Malaka.
- Review a text that mixes Tetun Dili textbook lines into a Belu request.
- Give two labelled snippets, Fehan spelling and Uma Lor spelling, without blending them.
- Write a neighbour reminder that leaves the time and place in Indonesian.
- Refuse a request to compose a new makoan speech.
- Separate a Dawan or Kupang Malay request from this skill.

## Register And Semantics

- Confirm Belu, Malaka, Fehan, Foho, or Tetun Dili before choosing words.
- `ita` in the Fehan sketch is both respectful you and inclusive we. Do not guess which from an isolated word.
- `Hi'i` is not interchangeable with `He'e`.
- Keep kinship calls inside the family pattern described in the Uma Lor study.
- Do not replace Indonesian grammar with a single Tetun noun and call the result Tetun.

## Review Checklist

- Is the requested variety Belu/Malaka Tetun, or is it Tetun Dili?
- Does any target-language sentence appear in `examples.md` or a user sample?
- Are Fehan apostrophe spellings mixed with Uma Lor spellings?
- Did `Hi'i` get assigned to a man or to a formal setting?
- Are Dawan or Kupang Malay words present?
- Are dates, prices, and places unchanged and still in Indonesian when no Tetun line covers them?
- Is adat wording quoted from a recording rather than newly composed?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: the message first, variety label in a short note.
- Review request: name the mismatch, then a safer mixed version.
- Public text: Indonesian for the facts, with at most one sourced Tetun opener.
