# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a chat or a short reminder, open with Iyo and put the errand in Indonesian. Add di'a only when the answer is the single word 'baik'. Name the missing dialect label.

## Sample Tasks

- Open a Flores neighbor note with the attested word for yes.
- Review a text that uses Sikka or Komodo words as if they were Manggarai.
- Explain lonto leok from the published gloss without writing the meeting script.
- Flag a newly composed go'et.
- Say which Peta Bahasa dialect names exist, and refuse to translate into an unnamed one.

## Register And Semantics

- Ask the village when the reader is an elder, a gendang meeting, or a public sign.
- Keep the Indonesian meaning before any Manggarai word is added.
- Skip jokes about Flores groups and skip invented adat lines.
- Leave medical, legal, and money terms in Indonesian.
- ema and ende are kinship words in the list, 'ayah' and 'ibu'. They are not universal polite titles for every stranger.
- lonto leok names a way of meeting. It does not replace the Indonesian sentence about when and where.

## Review Checklist

- Does the output use Indonesian grammar with one Manggarai word and call the whole line Manggarai?
- Is every Manggarai word in the local mirror or in a user sample?
- Is the dialect either named by the user or marked as unlabeled?
- Are Komodo, Sikka, and Lamaholot forms absent?
- Is any go'et a verbatim quotation, not a new composition?
- Are names, dates, and facts unchanged?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first, then the dialect note.
- Review request: name the risk, then a safer line.
- Public text: one Manggarai word plus Indonesian facts, and a speaker-review note.
