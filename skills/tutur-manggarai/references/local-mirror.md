# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Manggarai.
- ISO 639-3: `mqy`. Glottocode: `mang1405`. Glottolog lists it as a spoken L1 language, status developing, and points at Verheijen's dialect classification.
- Region: Flores, Nusa Tenggara Timur, across Manggarai Barat, Manggarai, and Manggarai Timur.
- Peta Bahasa: five dialects, Tangge, Manus, Rajong (Kesar), Kepo, and Rembong. Border note: Desa Mangalili is described as using Bima, Ende, and Manggarai. Do not flatten a border village into one language.
- Further dialect notes: Verheijen 1967, five groups and 43 subdialects. West Manggarai field study: Kempo, Kolang with an S to H correspondence, and a transition area. Sound pairs reported there include e/ea, i/ie, k/glottal, s/h, c/s, and h/gh.
- Grammars that exist and are not excerpted here: Troeboes et al. on Manggarai structure, and Semiun's grammar of the Kempo subdialect. Do not invent Kempo sentences from the fact that the thesis exists.
- Source confidence: Level 2 because Verheijen's dictionary work is a real dictionary. Everyday reuse is limited to the short list below.
- lonto leok: klikhijau glosses lonto as duduk and leok as melingkar or mengelilingi, so lonto leok is duduk melingkar. It is a meeting practice. The speeches inside it are not generated here.

Source links are in `sources.md`. This file does not copy a dictionary page.

## Local Usability

Use this file offline. A chat may start with Iyo and continue in Indonesian. That is mixed, and it is the safe ordinary pattern.

The wordlist has no dialect stamp. Using hau or di'a in a Kolang village, a Kempo village, or a Manggarai Timur village may be wrong even when the gloss is right for the list. Say "dialek tidak disebut di sumber kata" whenever those words appear.

People outside Flores often treat every Flores language as one. Komodo, Sikka, and Lamaholot are the three Peta Bahasa names to refuse. Ngada and Rongga are also separate languages in the NTT map and are not Manggarai.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Verified items only. Glosses follow the Wiktionary extract of Verheijen, Pulau Komodo: tanah, rakyat, dan bahasanya (Balai Pustaka, 1987), unless another source is named. Dialect: not stated.

- Iyo: 'iya'.
- di'a: 'baik'.
- toé: 'tidak'.
- hau: 'engkau'.
- ité: 'kita'.
- lonto: 'duduk'. The same gloss, duduk, is used for lonto in the lonto leok explanation.
- lako: 'berjalan'.
- ema: 'ayah'.
- ende: 'ibu'.
- ata: 'orang'.
- waé: 'air'.
- lonto leok: 'duduk melingkar', from the two-word gloss in the klikhijau article. Not a greeting by itself.

A published go'et, quoted only so it is not rewritten:

- "Muku ca pu'u neka woléng curup, téu ca ambo néka woléng lako."
- Gloss in the journal: pisang satu rumpun jangan beda bahasa, tebu satu rumpun jangan beda jalan.
- This is a fixed quoted line about land disputes and harmony. Do not shorten it into a caption, and do not write a new line in the same shape.

Do not add ritual titles, clan formulas, or steps of a lonto leok (who speaks, who concludes) beyond this note.

## Generation Rules

1. Write the meaning in plain Indonesian first.
2. Add only Manggarai pieces listed above or supplied by the user with a source and a dialect.
3. If a full sentence is not attested, keep it in Indonesian and label the message mixed.
4. State that the Verheijen extract is not dialect-labeled.
5. Do not compose go'et or lonto leok speeches.
6. Do not swap s and h to imitate Kolang or Kempo.
7. For public or professional text, keep time, place, price, and policy in Indonesian.
8. If the user names Komodo, Sikka, or Lamaholot, stop and say this skill is Manggarai only.
