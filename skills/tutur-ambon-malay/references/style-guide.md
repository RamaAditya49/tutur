# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Melayu Ambon.
- Also called Ambonese Malay. ISO 639-3 `abs`, glottocode `ambo1250`.
- It is a Malay contact variety used as a lingua franca. It is not bahasa tanah and not the Hitu/Asilulu language in Peta Bahasa.
- It is not Melayu Ternate and not Melayu Papua.
- Dictionary examples already mix Malay grammar with Indonesian vocabulary. That is the register to imitate, using whole attested lines.

## Safety

- Do not invent vocabulary, titles, or baileo formulas.
- Do not describe this variety as an untouched local language.
- Do not answer indigenous-language requests with `beta` and `ose`.
- Do not assume one pronoun of address fits every listener. The sources list both `ose` and `ale`.
- Keep medical, legal, and money details in Indonesian unless the dictionary sentence already contains them.

## Everyday

Cousin chat can be `Ale mo pi mana` or `Dangke lae su tolong beta tola oto ni`, with any new detail in Indonesian. `Beta masi bujang` is a sourced personal line, not a template for other life updates.

## Public

Use `Jang buang sampa di got` for a drain warning. Use `ingatang, baso tangang tu ee, supaya jang korona loko ose` only for the handwashing point the dictionary gives. Add bin locations or clinic hours in Indonesian.

## Professional

Spoken reminders may use `basudara samua, mar biar kata dalam oto lai musti pasang masker e` when the topic is masks in a vehicle. Rosters, invoices, and official letters stay in Indonesian.

## Adat Boundary

The dictionary mentions `berang` and `baileo` while defining the cloth worn because a ritual is underway. That sentence describes an event. It does not license new ritual speech, titles, or the order of a ceremony.
