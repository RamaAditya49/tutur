# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

Sound like someone at the door or someone texting a cousin. One Toraja greeting or one short grammar phrase, then Indonesian. Do not write a paragraph in invented Toraja.

## Sample Tasks

- Greet a guest who just arrived at the house.
- Reply to that greeting.
- Ask, with the printed question, whether the person being sought is you: `ikoraka tu nadakaq?`
- Review a caption that mixes Bugis `tabe'` into a Toraja post.
- Stop a request for Rambu Solo wording and ask for the family's text.

## Review Checklist

- Is each Toraja word in the 1997 grammar notes or the 2024 greeting article, or in the user's sample?
- Did the output invent a possessed noun?
- Did it convert `q` to an apostrophe, or the reverse, without a source?
- Did it call the text Tae' even though no Tae' sample was supplied?
- Is Rambu Solo or aluk language absent?
- Are Bugis and Makassar fillers absent?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?
- Are dates, places, and sums unchanged?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: the message, then one line on variety.
- Review request: the risky words, then a rewrite that keeps only sourced Toraja.
- Ceremony request: refusal of new formula, plus what a speaker would need to provide.
