# Sources

Language: Toraja, catalogued as Toraja-Sa'dan

Region/context: Sulawesi Selatan. Peta Bahasa names Kota Makassar, Tana Toraja, Toraja Utara, Luwu, and Luwu Utara, with eleven dialects. This skill does not collapse those dialects into Tae'.

## Source Confidence

Level 2. Tata Bahasa Toraja (1997) is a published grammar from the regional language project, and it supports the short grammatical examples here. The visit greeting is taken from a 2024 journal article that glosses it in Indonesian. Raise the level only after a dialect-tagged corpus or an opened dictionary is added.

Not copied: J. Tammu and H. van der Veen, Kamus Toradja-Indonesia, Jajasan Perguruan Kristen Toradja, 1972. Its existence is reported in later Kamus-digitalisasi papers. No entry from that book is reproduced here.

## Editorial Position

Everyday text may open with the published greeting or a short 1997 grammar phrase, then continue in Indonesian. Dialect labels stay as the user gave them. Rambu Solo, aluk, and other ceremony formulas are not generated. The 2024 article mentions Aluk Todolo as historical context for who used the greeting across religious lines. That mention is not a license to write aluk language.

Peta Bahasa says Toraja differs by about 81–100 percent from other South Sulawesi languages it compared, including Bugis. Do not fill gaps with Bugis or Makassar words.
