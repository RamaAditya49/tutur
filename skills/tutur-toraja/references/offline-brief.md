# Offline Brief

## Local Facts

- Purpose: ordinary Toraja-aware chat, reminders, and review.
- Catalog: Toraja-Sa'dan, ISO 639-3 `sda`, glottocode `tora1261`. Alternative names include Tae', Ta'e, Sa'dan, Toraja.
- Badan Bahasa name: Bahasa Toraja. Eleven dialects. Sak (Sa') and Tae is the Luwu / Luwu Utara dialect, not the whole language.
- Other dialect labels on that map include Toraja Pandang, Karadeng, Mangkendek, Saluputi, Makale, Rindinggalo, Sanggalangi, Sesean, Sangalla, and Toala.
- Grammar sample: Tata Bahasa Toraja, 1997. Village dialect of those examples is not stated on the pages used here.
- Greeting sample: `manasumo raka?` in Welem 2024, glossed as a visit greeting, literally about whether the rice is cooked.
- Orthography split: 1997 grammar uses `q` for the glottal. The greeting article uses an apostrophe.
- Level 2 for short sourced phrases. Not a full translator.
- This file does not copy the 1972 dictionary.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent vocabulary.
- Do not write Rambu Solo, aluk, or other ritual formulas.
- Do not call every sentence Tae' or every sentence Sa'dan.
- Do not mix Bugis or Makassar particles into the line.
- Do not convert `q` spelling into apostrophe spelling, or the reverse, without showing both sources.
- Keep times, places, prices, and instructions in Indonesian.
- If the sentence is not attested, leave it in Indonesian and label the message mixed.
- Possessive suffixes below are only for the nouns printed with them. Do not build new possessed nouns.

## Safe Output Patterns

- Open with `manasumo raka?` when someone is arriving at a house.
- Use `io manasumo` only as the published reply to that greeting.
- Use one grammar phrase (`mukande`, `puntingku`, `banuammu`) when it matches, then Indonesian.
- For a funeral or aluk request, ask for the family's text. Do not compose one.
