# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language name in this skill: Bahasa Toraja, catalogued as Toraja-Sa'dan.
- ISO 639-3: `sda`. Glottocode: `tora1261`.
- Alternative names on the Glottolog entry: Tae', Ta'e, Sa'dan, Toraja, Toradja, Rantepao (as a WALS other-name), South Toraja.
- Badan Bahasa: Bahasa Toraja, eleven dialects, difference among dialects about 51–80 percent. Sak (Sa') and Tae is dialect 10 on that list.
- Examples from Tata Bahasa Toraja (1997) are Bahasa Toraja grammar examples without a village tag in the extracted pages.
- Greeting examples are from Welem 2024 and are ordinary visit language.
- Source confidence: Level 2.
- Links are listed in `sources.md`.

Source mirror notes:

- Do not describe this file as a Tae' textbook. Tae' is one documented dialect name, not the provenance label of every line below.
- The 1972 Toradja–Indonesia dictionary is a real book. It is not mirrored here.
- Funeral and aluk wording is outside this mirror.

## Local Usability

Use the greeting when the scene is a visit or a guest at the door. Use the grammar snippets for possession, a question, or a single verb gloss. A complete announcement, a worship text, or a speech for the dead is not assembled from these pieces.

The speech habit to copy is the doorstep question about rice, plus endings that mark whose thing it is. A string of Indonesian sentences with an extra particle is not Bahasa Toraja. Greetings that are not printed in the 1997 grammar or the 2024 article stay out of this list.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

From Tata Bahasa Toraja (1997), forms that remain readable in the extracted text:

- `aku` — aku.
- `iko` — engkau. Printed inside `si-+ iko` and in the question below.
- `ikoraka tu nadakaq?` — engkaulah yang dicarinya?
- `pirara tu anakmu?` — berapakah anakmu itu?
- `kande` — makan. `mu- + kande` — `mukande`, engkau makan.
- `banua` — rumah. `banuammu` — rumahmu. `banuanna` — rumahnya.
- `punti` — pisang. `puntingku` — pisang saya.
- `lima tu anakna` — anak sudah lima. `limamo tu anakna` — anaknya sudah lima.
- `misaq` — satu. `simisaq` — masing-masing satu.
- `tedong` — kerbau. `tedongmi` — kerbau bapak, with the respectful possessive in that example.
- `kada` — kata. `kadammi` — kata anda.
- `boqboq` — nasi, as the object gloss of `ungkande (boqboq)`.
- `doiq` — uang, in `mekadoiq` "meminta uang".
- `bayu` — baju. The derived form on that line is garbled in the extract, so only `bayu` "baju" is used.
- `-ku` — milik orang pertama tunggal, as in `puntingku`.
- `-mu` — milik orang kedua, as in `banuammu` and `anakmu`.
- `-mi` — the grammar's respectful second-person possessive in `tedongmi` "kerbau bapak" and `kadammi` "kata anda".
- `-na` — milik orang ketiga, as in `banuanna` "rumahnya".
- `-ki` — orang pertama jamak in the printed glosses `bayungki` "baju kami" and `banuangki` "rumah kami".
- `-ta` — printed on `eranta` "tangga kita", `puntinta` "pisang kita", `banuanta` "rumah kita". The section label in the scan is hard to read and also gestures at a respectful second person. Follow the example glosses, and do not add a new rule.
- `-mo` — in `limamo tu anakna`, the grammar calls this perfective: the five children are already counted.
- `-raka` — question clitic, parallel to Indonesian `-kah`, in `ikoraka`.

From Welem 2024, ordinary greeting, not a ceremony formula:

- `manasumo raka?` — apakah nasi sudah matang? The article also compresses it to apakah sudah masak/matang. It is used to greet, including across religious lines.
- `io manasumo` — ya, sudah matang. Printed as the reply.
- `manasumo ta lendu' opa` — sudah masak, mari singgah. The same article also prints `lendu'opa` without the space. Keep the spaced form when using the "mari singgah" gloss, and do not invent a third spelling.

The article's cooking note writes `unnasu bo'bo' / ma'nasu` while explaining menanak nasi. That is a parenthetical, not a second dictionary entry. The grammar's word for nasi in the cited line is `boqboq`. Leave the two spellings in their own sources.

## Generation Rules

1. Put the practical meaning in Indonesian first.
2. Add Toraja only from the lists above or from a sample the user supplies.
3. Do not attach `-ku`, `-mu`, `-mi`, `-na`, `-ki`, or `-ta` to a noun that is not printed with that suffix.
4. Do not merge `boqboq` and `bo'bo'` into one made-up spelling.
5. Name the variety limit: Bahasa Toraja / Toraja-Sa'dan, dialect unknown unless the user named one.
6. Label mixed text as mixed.
7. Refuse new Rambu Solo and aluk sentences.

## Register Handling

- Everyday visit: `manasumo raka?`, then Indonesian.
- Reply at the door: `io manasumo` or `manasumo ta lendu' opa`, then Indonesian if more is needed.
- Respectful possession: only the printed `-mi` examples, not a new honorific system.
- Public and admin: Indonesian for the operative sentence.
- Adat and funeral: review-only. No composed formula.
