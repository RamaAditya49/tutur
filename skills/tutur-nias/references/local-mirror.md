# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Nias. Endonym in Peta Bahasa and in the Kemendikbud reader: Li Niha.
- ISO 639-3: `nia`. Glottocode: `nias1242`.
- Region: Nias, Nias Selatan, Kota Gunungsitoli, and Pulau-Pulau Batu as mapped by Peta Bahasa.
- Four dialects in Peta Bahasa: Simaluaya, Pasar Teluk Dalam, Hilimboe, and Nias Utara (Olora and Pasar Lahewa).
- Separated by Peta Bahasa from Batak and from the other languages named in that comparison.
- Source confidence: Level 2. Kamus Nias-Indonesia (1985) and a pictured learner dictionary are in the Kemendikdasmen repository. This mirror does not copy them.
- The greeting and the three names below are from the Kemendikbud reader Ya'ahowu (bacaan SMA, seri sastrawan 3T).
- The thanks sentence is from a Pengadilan Tinggi Agama Medan report of speech at PA Gunungsitoli, with the office's own Indonesian gloss.
- Source links are in `sources.md`. This file does not copy a dictionary.

## Local Usability

Use this file offline. Ordinary chat may open with Ya'ahowu and then switch to Indonesian. The reader says the same word is the reply, in town and in a village, to a child or an elder, at ordinary volume. That is a greeting habit, not a script for adat.

Do not invent Li Niha for a second sentence. Dialectometry in Peta Bahasa already shows the four dialects far enough apart that one spelling should not be forced on all of them.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Verified items only. Glosses follow the cited source.

From the Kemendikbud reader Ya'ahowu:

- Ya'ahowu: the word to say when meeting Ono Niha, answered with the same word. The reader sets it beside salam sejahtera and similar greetings. It does not print a morpheme split.
- Ono Niha: orang Nias.
- Tano Niha: Tanah Nias.
- Li Niha: bahasa Nias. Peta Bahasa says the same: orang Nias call this language Li Niha.
- afo: sirih pinang offered to chew when people meet. The reader also says afo is the first dish at sacred adat events. Do not write that serving order as a new formula. The gloss "sirih pinang" is the usable part.
- Greeting manner in the same chapter: one right hand, no bow, no hand-kiss, no cheek kiss, ordinary volume. This is a description of the greeting, not a ritual text.

From the PTA Medan news note, quoted speech at PA Gunungsitoli:

- "Saohagolo Ama…saohagolo ma andro khomi fefu"
- Office gloss: "terima kasih Pak..terimakasih kami ucapkan kepada Bapak semuanya".
- Quote the sentence whole. Do not cut it into a new thanks frame. The spelling in the news note is Saohagolo, without the ö some community pages use.

Do not add wedding speeches, hoho lines, or healing formulas.

## Generation Rules

1. Write the meaning in plain Indonesian first.
2. Add only Nias pieces listed above or supplied by the user with a source.
3. If a full sentence is not in the reader or the court note, keep it in Indonesian and label the message mixed.
4. Say that dialect is unspecified unless the user names Simaluaya, Teluk Dalam, Hilimboe, or Nias Utara.
5. Do not borrow Batak or Mandailing greetings.
6. For public or professional text, keep time, place, price, and policy in Indonesian.
7. Refuse ceremony, healing, and wedding formulas even when the user asks for "just a short adat line".
