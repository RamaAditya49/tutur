# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Nias, Li Niha.
- Folder: tutur-nias.
- ISO 639-3 `nia`. Glottocode `nias1242`.
- People: Ono Niha. Place name in the reader: Tano Niha.
- Area: the four Peta Bahasa dialects. Simaluaya and Pasar Teluk Dalam are in Nias Selatan. Hilimboe is in Susua. Nias Utara in that map is Olora and Pasar Lahewa.
- Not this skill: Batak Toba, Mandailing, or a single "bahasa Nias" spelling for every village.
- Speech habit to name: Ya'ahowu, answered with Ya'ahowu, one right hand, then the practical point.

## Safety Rules

- Do not invent vocabulary, ritual language, or kinship titles.
- Do not let Gunungsitoli town speech stand for Teluk Dalam or the Batu islands.
- Do not write adat, legal, or official text from a greeting line.
- Keep Indonesian when the Li Niha form is unknown.
- Ask for a sample when the user names a desa and wants local spelling.
- Do not mix Batak into a Nias line.

## Practical Use

Use this skill for source-aware Nias openings and review of machine text. It fits chat and short community notes. It does not fit a Li Niha-only announcement or a ceremony script.

## Registers

### Everyday

Attested opening from the Kemendikbud reader:

> Ya'ahowu

The other person replies Ya'ahowu. The errand after it stays in Indonesian. Mark the note mixed.

### Public

Use Ya'ahowu, then Indonesian for the time and place. Do not claim the notice is southern or northern Nias unless the user names the dialect. Mark the notice mixed.

### Professional

Admin text keeps deadlines, money, and document names in Indonesian. The court thanks sentence may be quoted only when its gloss fits: thanks to a man, addressed as Ama / Pak, on behalf of a group. A new office email does not get a freshly cut saohagolo sentence.

### Adat boundary

Do not write wedding lines, hoho, healing formulas, or the order of a sacred afo serving. The reader mentions afo at sacred events. That mention is a warning, not a template. Ask for the text the family already uses.
