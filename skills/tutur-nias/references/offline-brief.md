# Offline Brief

## Local Facts

- Purpose: ordinary messages, the Ya'ahowu greeting, and review for Bahasa Nias (Li Niha).
- Region: Peta Bahasa names Desa Simaluaya (Pulau-Pulau Batu, Nias Selatan), Pasar Teluk Dalam, Hilimboe (Susua), Olora (Gunung Sitoli Utara), and Pasar Lahewa.
- Dialects in that source: (1) Simaluaya, (2) Pasar Teluk Dalam, (3) Hilimboe, (4) Nias Utara at Olora and Pasar Lahewa. Difference about 51%–69%.
- Peta Bahasa: the Nias isolek is a language, about 81%–100% apart from Batak, Jawa, Gayo, Minangkabau, and Melayu.
- Speakers call the language Li Niha. The Kemendikbud reader also uses Ono Niha for orang Nias and Tano Niha for Tanah Nias.
- ISO 639-3: `nia`. Glottocode: `nias1242`. Glottolog status note: developing, not endangered.
- Source confidence: Level 2 because Kamus Nias-Indonesia (1985) exists. Quoted lines in this skill are from the reader and one court news gloss, not from unread dictionary pages.
- Speech habit: say Ya'ahowu, hear Ya'ahowu back. One right hand. No bow, no cheek kiss. Then the errand, often in Indonesian.
- Attested lines to reuse are copied in `local-mirror.md`. They are not a license to translate a paragraph.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent vocabulary, kinship titles, or ritual lines.
- Do not treat Nias Utara and Nias Selatan as one dialect.
- Do not write wedding, hoho, fangowai, or healing speeches from a greeting.
- Keep Indonesian when the Li Niha word is missing.
- Do not answer a Nias request with Batak Toba or Mandailing.
- Afo is sirih pinang in the reader. Mention it only as that gloss. Do not script how it is served at a sacred event.
- Ask which area the reader is from before a public line.

## Safe Output Patterns

- Open with Ya'ahowu, then give the errand in Indonesian. Mark the message mixed.
- Use Ono Niha, Tano Niha, and Li Niha only with the glosses in the reader.
- Quote the court thanks sentence whole when thanks must be in Li Niha. Otherwise use Indonesian terima kasih.
- For review, flag southern and northern forms mixed without a label.
- For unknown terms, keep Indonesian and name the gap.
- For public use, ask a Li Niha speaker from the target dialect.
- For admin facts, keep time, price, and document names in Indonesian.
- Mark mixed lines as mixed.
