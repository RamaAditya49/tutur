# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a chat or a short reminder, open with Ya'ahowu and put the errand in Indonesian. If thanks must be in Li Niha, quote the court sentence whole and only when the addressee matches "Pak" and "Bapak semuanya". Otherwise thank them in Indonesian.

## Sample Tasks

- Open a Nias chat with Ya'ahowu, then the pickup time in Indonesian.
- Review a text that uses a Batak greeting as if it were Li Niha.
- Explain why Lahewa and Teluk Dalam are not one dialect in Peta Bahasa.
- Write a community reminder that stays mixed.
- Refuse a request for a wedding or healing formula.

## Register And Semantics

- The reader says Ya'ahowu is used across age and status for an ordinary meeting. That does not license an adat speech.
- Keep the Indonesian meaning before any Li Niha word is added.
- Skip jokes about Ono Niha, and skip invented adat lines.
- Leave medical, legal, and money terms in Indonesian.
- afo is a named object. Do not turn the reader's adat remark into a serving script.
- The court sentence is one published utterance. Do not mine it for loose words.

## Review Checklist

- Does the output use Indonesian grammar with one Nias word and call the whole line Li Niha?
- Is every Nias word in the local mirror or in a user sample?
- Is the dialect limit stated?
- Is a wedding, hoho, or healing request left unwritten?
- Are names, dates, and facts unchanged?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?
- Is the thanks sentence quoted whole, or omitted?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first, then the source note.
- Review request: name the risk, then a safer line.
- Public text: Ya'ahowu plus Indonesian facts, and a speaker-review note.
