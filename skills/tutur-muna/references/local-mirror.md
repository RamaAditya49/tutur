# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Muna. Endonym in the grammar: `wamba Wuna`.
- ISO 639-3: `mnb`. Glottocode: `muna1247`.
- Variety: standard Muna, Kecamatan Katobu (Raha), from van den Berg's grammar.
- Region on Peta Bahasa: Pulau Muna and the west coast of Pulau Buton.
- Source confidence: Level 2.
- Dictionaries known but not excerpted: Mattalitti 1985 and the 2023 Kantor Bahasa Sulawesi Tenggara dictionary.
- Links: `sources.md`.

Source mirror notes:

- Van den Berg: square brackets mark an infix or a nasal prefix. `na-k[um]ala` means "he will go", with `-um-` marked, not spelled as brackets.
- The infix `-um-` goes after the first consonant of the root in the regular case he describes.
- `ne` is the locative in the street greeting. `we` is the locative in the Raha example below. Both are printed. They are not free substitutes.
- `tehi` is given as the word for sea and, separately, as homophonous with afraid. Do not use it until the intended sense is one of those two, and do not build a new sentence with it.

## Local Usability

The usable habit is a short directional greeting, a noon remark, or a first-person line about going or learning. A village notice, a school form, or a ritual text is not translated from this list. In Raha, van den Berg already notes that Indonesian dominates in many families. Mixed messages match that town more closely than a fully invented Muna paragraph would.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Lines from the grammar, with its English gloss and an Indonesian rendering for this skill:

- `wamba Wuna` — the Muna language. Indonesian: bahasa Muna. Related: `miendo Wuna` the people, `witeno Wuna` the island, `wuna` flower.
- `o-k[um]ala ne hamai?` — where are you going? Marked as the usual greeting on the street. Indonesian: kamu mau ke mana?
- `ka-ghindotu-no gholeo itu-a!` — the sun is at its height. Marked as a greeting at noon. Indonesian: matahari sudah di puncak.
- `a-po-guru-ghoo wamba Wuna` — I am learning Muna. Indonesian: saya belajar bahasa Muna.
- `naewine a-k[um]ala we Raha` — tomorrow I will go to Raha. Indonesian: besok saya pergi ke Raha. Locative here is `we`, not `ne`.
- `hamai-ko-mo?` — where are you? Indonesian: kamu di mana?
- `o hae-no ini-a?` — what is this? Indonesian: apa ini?
- `peda hae itu-a?` — how is it (going)? Indonesian: bagaimana keadaan ini?
- `sehae taghu umuru-nto?` — how old are you (polite)? Indonesian: umur Anda berapa tahun?

Person marking is visible inside those lines, not as a table to extend:

- `a-` in `a-po-guru-ghoo` and `a-k[um]ala` for saya.
- `o-` in `o-k[um]ala` for kamu on the verb.
- `-ko` in `hamai-ko-mo?` for kamu on "where".
- `-nto` in `umuru-nto` for the polite "your" in the age question.

`o` before `hae` is the article in `o hae-no ini-a?`. Do not put it on random Indonesian nouns.

The grammar also prints `wamba Wolio` while talking about people who know Wolio. That line names the other language. It is not a Muna synonym.

## Generation Rules

1. Write the point in Indonesian.
2. If a grammar line already says it, use that line unchanged, including hyphens and brackets.
3. Tell the reader, once, that the brackets mark an infix.
4. Do not attach `a-` or `o-` to a new verb.
5. Do not move `we Raha` to `ne Raha`, or `ne hamai` to `we hamai`.
6. Name the variety: standard Muna, Katobu, when the example is one of these.
7. Label mixed text as mixed.
8. Refuse new adat formulas.

## Register Handling

- Road and noon: the two greetings above.
- Learning or travel: the `a-` lines, then Indonesian.
- Polite age question: only the printed `umuru-nto` line.
- Public and admin: Indonesian for the operative facts.
- Gumas, Tiworo, and Buton dialect points: do not present Katobu lines as local transcripts.
- Adat: ask for a community text.
