# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Endonym: `wamba Wuna`. Indonesian name: Bahasa Muna.
- ISO 639-3: `mnb`. Glottocode: `muna1247`.
- Variety for the examples: standard Muna, Kecamatan Katobu, including Raha, as described by van den Berg.
- Not Wolio. Not Tolaki. Not Kulisusu. Not Cia-Cia.
- Gumas (Gu, Mawasangka) and Tiworo are Muna varieties he separates from the Katobu examples. Peta Bahasa's Burhanuddin list is a second classification. Do not blend the labels.

## Safety Rules

- Do not invent vocabulary or verb prefixes.
- Do not strip the grammar's hyphens and brackets into a new popular spelling.
- Keep medical, legal, school, and government details in Indonesian.
- Ask whether the reader is in Raha, inland Katobu, southern Muna, or Buton before a public text.
- A sentence that is not in the grammar stays in Indonesian.

## Registers

### Everyday

On the road, `o-k[um]ala ne hamai?` At noon, `ka-ghindotu-no gholeo itu-a!` To a cousin about tomorrow, `naewine a-k[um]ala we Raha`, then Indonesian.

### Public

A neighborhood note can open with the street greeting or the noon greeting. What people must do, and when, stays in Indonesian. Say the Muna line is standard Muna from the Katobu grammar.

### Professional

Office and school text stays in Indonesian. `a-po-guru-ghoo wamba Wuna` fits a class announcement about learning the language, not a translated circular.

### Adat

Do not generate ritual, wedding, or customary law formulas. If a family text is pasted, mark any word that is not in the list above and leave the ceremony language untouched.
