# Offline Brief

## Local Facts

- Purpose: ordinary messages, adaptation, and review for Bahasa Tolaki.
- Region: Kolaka, Kolaka Utara, Konawe, Konawe Utara, Konawe Selatan, Kolaka Timur, and parts of Kota Kendari.
- ISO 639-3 `lbw`. Glottocode `tola1247`. Bungku-Tolaki, not Muna, not Wolio.
- Source confidence: Level 2. Dictionary and function-word study exist. Phrases in this skill come from glossed papers, not from a copied dictionary.
- Peta Bahasa lists six dialects: Mekongga, Rahambuu, Kodeoha, Konawe, Laromerui, and Waru. Differences about 56.78%–75.54%.
- Older literature cited by Peta Bahasa also uses the name Tolaki Laiwui, with Tolaki and Laiwui as two dialects. Do not invent a third label.
- Tolaki in Kolaka and Kolaka Utara is spoken beside Bugis. In Konawe Utara and Konawe it is spoken beside Culambacu. In Konawe Selatan it is spoken beside Sunda, Jawa, and Bali communities. Those contact languages are not Tolaki.
- Polite requests in the Dirjan and Ino study open with `tabee` or `tabea`.
- Family address in the Haslan study: `ina` ibu, `ama` bapak, `pue` / `mbue` kakek, nenek, or cucu, `ka'aka` kakak, `hai` adik, `ana` anak.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent vocabulary, ritual lines, noble titles, or adat formulas.
- Do not treat one dialect as the whole language.
- Do not use `tabea inggomiu` as a universal honorific. The same paper says the phrase fits elder-to-elder talk and does not stay polite when an elder aims it at a child.
- Keep Indonesian when the Tolaki word is not in this brief or in a user sample.
- Ask which community when the user names a village or a noble versus non-noble setting. A Wawotobi study describes different family address for those two groups. Do not generate the noble set from this skill.
- Label mixed Tolaki-Indonesian text as mixed.
- Public, legal, school, and adat text needs a Tolaki speaker.

## Sample Prompts

- "Use $tutur-tolaki to review this Bahasa Tolaki text."
- "Make a source-aware Bahasa Tolaki version of this short message for a cousin in Konawe."
- "Check whether this is Tolaki or just Indonesian with a Muna or Wolio word."
- "Write a neighborhood reminder that opens with tabee and keeps the time in Indonesian."
- "Review an adat welcome. Do not invent the ritual lines."

## Safe Output Patterns

- Open with `tabea` or `tabee`, then the practical point in Indonesian.
- For thanks or a passed greeting, `salamunggu toka` is the glossed line for "saya titip salam". Do not stretch it into a new idiom.
- For family chat, `ina`, `ama`, `ka'aka`, and `hai` may be used as address words with the table glosses.
- For unknown terms, leave Indonesian and name the gap.
- For adat, ask for the local text instead of composing one.
