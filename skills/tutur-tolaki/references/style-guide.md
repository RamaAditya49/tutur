# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Tolaki. Older label in the Peta Bahasa note: Tolaki Laiwui.
- ISO 639-3 `lbw`. Glottocode `tola1247`.
- Place: Kolaka, Kolaka Utara, Konawe, Konawe Utara, Konawe Selatan, Kolaka Timur, parts of Kota Kendari.
- Not Bahasa Muna. Not Bahasa Wolio. Not Culambacu. Not Bugis.

## Safety

- Do not invent vocabulary, titles, or adat formulas.
- Do not treat Mekongga, Rahambuu, Kodeoha, Konawe, Laromerui, and Waru as one spelling.
- Do not aim `tabea inggomiu` downward at a child.
- Do not generate noble kinship address from a commoner sample.
- Keep Indonesian when the Tolaki item is not glossed in this skill or supplied by the user.
- Label mixed text as mixed.

## Everyday

Short, like a text to a cousin. Open with `tabee` or `tabea`. Use `ina`, `ama`, `ka'aka`, or `hai` only as the kin term the table records. The rest of the plan stays Indonesian.

`Tina, embee ariamu?` stays the published question. Do not change Tina's errand.

## Public

One Tolaki opener, then Indonesian for time, place, and what to bring. `salamunggu toka` only when the meaning is "saya titip salam", which is the published gloss.

Printed village notices need a local speaker if any sentence beyond the opener is supposed to be Tolaki.

## Professional

Admin, school, clinic, and office facts stay in Indonesian: deadlines, document names, money, and privacy. A `tabea` at the start is enough local signal. Do not translate a KTP request into unattested Tolaki.

## Adat Boundary

Dirjan and Ino note that adat events are where the most polite Tolaki is expected, from elders. That observation is not a script. Do not compose ritual lines, marriage speech, or noble formulas. Ask for the community's own wording. If the user pastes a line, review it against the glossed pieces in this skill and mark everything else as unchecked.
