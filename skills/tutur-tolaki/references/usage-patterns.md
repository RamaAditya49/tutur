# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a cousin chat, a guest line, or a neighborhood reminder, return the message first. Open with `tabea` or `tabee`. Keep time, place, and money in Indonesian. Say that the result is mixed when both languages are in it.

## Sample Tasks

- Rewrite a short Indonesian note with a Tolaki permission opener.
- Review a text that claims to be Tolaki and flag Muna or Wolio material.
- Produce two tones: peer `tabee`, and elder-to-elder `tabea inggomiu`, without pretending the rest is Tolaki.
- Localize a family message using `ina`, `ama`, `ka'aka`, or `hai` with the table glosses.
- Turn a village reminder into Tolaki-aware Indonesian.
- Stop an adat request and ask for the speaker's own line.

## Register And Semantics

- Confirm who speaks to whom before using `tabea inggomiu`.
- Keep names and place names unchanged.
- Do not use noble address unless the user supplies it.
- Do not treat Mekongga wording as Konawe wording.
- Technical, legal, and medical terms stay in Indonesian.
- A single polite word does not make a paragraph Tolaki.

## Review Checklist

- Does the text mix Indonesian syntax with one Tolaki word and call the whole thing Tolaki?
- Is every Tolaki item in `local-mirror.md` or in a user sample?
- Is the dialect named, or is the text honest that the sample is not dialect-labeled?
- Are `ina`, `ama`, `pue`, `ka'aka`, and `hai` used with the published kin gloss, not a guessed one?
- Is `tabea inggomiu` aimed at a peer or an elder, not downward at a child?
- Are dates, places, and duties still in Indonesian?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?
- Did the answer avoid Muna and Wolio?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first, one-line note on what is attested.
- Review request: name the unsupported stretch, then a shorter rewrite.
- Public text: Tolaki opener, Indonesian facts, speaker-review note if it will be printed.
