# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a chat or a short reminder, open with santabi and put the errand in Indonesian. For more than one listener, the dictionary sentence is "santabi di hamu sude". Thanks, if needed, is the noun mauliate, not a new paragraph. A younger sibling in a quoted sentence is anggi.

## Sample Tasks

- Open a Mandailing family chat with santabi, then the pickup detail in Indonesian.
- Review a text that uses Toba horas as if it were the Mandailing hello.
- Check whether a dictionary word is marked Akl, Mdl, or shared.
- Write a neighborhood reminder that stays mixed.
- Refuse a horja or markobar request and say what would be needed instead: a text the family already uses.

## Register And Semantics

- Ask whether the listener is one person or a group before using "di hamu sude".
- Keep the Indonesian meaning before any Mandailing word is added.
- Skip jokes about Batak subgroups, and skip invented adat lines.
- Leave medical, legal, and money terms in Indonesian.
- anggi in the sweeping example is adik, not a general word for every friend.
- Code-mixing is the safe shape. Do not "complete" a mixed note into a fake full translation.

## Review Checklist

- Does the output use Indonesian grammar with one Mandailing word and call the whole line Mandailing?
- Is every Mandailing word in the local mirror or in a user sample?
- Is an Akl-only entry absent?
- Is horas used only as the dictionary's selamat wish, not as a Toba hello?
- Are names, dates, and facts unchanged?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?
- Is a horja or markobar request left unwritten?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first, then the source note.
- Review request: name the risk, then a safer line.
- Public text: santabi plus Indonesian facts, and a speaker-review note.
