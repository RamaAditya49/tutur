# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Mandailing. Glottolog: Batak Mandailing.
- Folder: tutur-mandailing.
- ISO 639-3 `btm`. Glottocode `bata1291`.
- Area for examples: Mandailing Natal speech, using shared or Mdl lines from the Angkola-Mandailing dictionary.
- Not this skill: Batak Toba, Karo, Simalungun, Pakpak, and entries the dictionary marks Akl.
- Peta Bahasa still files Mandailing as a dialect of Bahasa Batak. The ISO entry does not. Say both facts when identity is the question.
- Speech habit to name: santabi, then the point. anggi for the younger sibling in ordinary dictionary sentences. horas means selamat in this dictionary, not "hello" in the Toba way.

## Safety Rules

- Do not invent vocabulary, ritual language, or kinship titles.
- Do not let a shared dictionary line stand for every Mandailing desa.
- Do not write adat, legal, or official text from a greeting line.
- Keep Indonesian when the Mandailing form is unknown.
- Ask for a sample when the user names Kotanopan, Panyabungan, Natal, or a border village and wants local spelling.
- Do not mix Toba into a Mandailing line.

## Practical Use

Use this skill for source-aware Mandailing openings and review of machine text. It fits chat and short community notes. It does not fit a Mandailing-only announcement or a horja script.

## Registers

### Everyday

Attested group opening from the dictionary:

> santabi di hamu sude

That line means "salam hormat untuk kalian semua". The errand after it stays in Indonesian. For one cousin, "santabi" is the same lemma, and the rest of the text is Indonesian. Mark that note mixed.

### Public

Use the group santabi line, then Indonesian for the time and place. Do not turn a RT reminder into a horja announcement. Mark the notice mixed.

### Professional

Admin text keeps deadlines, money, and document names in Indonesian. mauliate may close a short note as the glossed thanks word. Do not invent an office formula around it.

### Adat boundary

Horja, markobar, and patibal sere are not written here. The dictionary glosses adat as adat and gives institutional examples. Those glosses are not a script. Ask for the text the family or the panitia already uses.
