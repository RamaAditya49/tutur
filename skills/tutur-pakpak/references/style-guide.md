# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Pakpak. Dictionary title: Dairi Pakpak. Glottolog: Batak Dairi, ISO 639-3 `btd`, glottocode `bata1294`.
- Not Karo. Not Batak Toba. Not Simalungun.
- Five suak in the USU Press book: Sim-sim, Keppas, Pegagan, Kelasen, Boang.
- Phrases in this skill: sentences and glosses from Aksara Pakpak, USU Press, 2023. The 1977 Manik dictionary is the Level 2 dictionary and was not quoted line by line.

## Safety Rules

- Do not invent vocabulary.
- Do not invent kinship titles or a merga formula.
- Do not treat `tulan` in the bone example as an uncle title.
- Keep the two e vowels apart when a minimal pair is quoted.
- Keep medical, legal, school, and government details in Indonesian.
- A full sentence that is not in the 2023 book stays untranslated.

## Registers

### Everyday

Open with `njuah-njuah`. A group gets `njuah-njuah mo kita karinana`. Then Indonesian. `Ko` is the you in `ulang ko menangkih-nagkih i benna terutung`. Use that sentence only when someone might actually climb the tree.

`Mella` means kalau. `Mela` means malu. A text that swaps them says the wrong thing.

### Public

A reminder can start with `njuah-njuah mo kita karinana`. If the bridge is really out, the book has `enggo nceda jembaten, oda boi ke karap i`. The detour, the hour, and who to call stay Indonesian. Name the suak if the notice is for one valley.

### Professional

Admin, school, and clinic messages stay in Indonesian. `Njuah-njuah` at the top is enough if the office wants a local opening. Do not translate the form, the fee, or the deadline.

### Adat

Do not write a menutung tulan utterance, a pustaka reading, or an aksara Pakpak line. The book says the script was cut on bark and stone, and that only someone who knows the language can write it. Ask that person. The old month-day list in the same chapter is not a calendar to paste into a poster.
