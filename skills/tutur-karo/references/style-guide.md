# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Karo, also cakap Karo in the 2001 dictionary example. ISO 639-3 `btx`, glottocode `bata1293`.
- Not Batak Toba. Not Simalungun. Not Pakpak.
- Peta Bahasa calls it a dialect of Bahasa Batak. This skill still does not borrow Toba wording from that label.
- Phrases in this skill: readable glosses from Kamus Bahasa Karo-Indonesia (2001).

## Safety Rules

- Do not invent vocabulary.
- Do not invent merga, kalimbubu, or kinship address.
- Do not repair OCR into a new sentence.
- Keep medical, legal, school, and government details in Indonesian.
- A full sentence that is not readable in the source stays untranslated.

## Registers

### Everyday

Open a chat with `mejuah-juah`. For several people, the dictionary example is `mejuah-juah kita kerina`. The request itself, the hour, and the place stay Indonesian. Thanks, if needed, is `bujur`.

`ula ngandung` belongs in talk about a child crying, because that is the dictionary context. Do not reuse it as a general "don't".

### Public

A reminder can start with `mejuah-juah kita kerina` and end with `bujur`. What people must bring, and when, stays Indonesian. Do not add a clan greeting to make it sound more Karo.

### Professional

Admin, school, and clinic messages stay in Indonesian. `Mejuah-juah` at the top is enough if the office wants a local opening. Do not translate the form, the fee, or the deadline.

### Adat

Do not write ritual Karo, a merga introduction, or a wedding formula. Ask the family or the orang yang memegang adat for the wording they use. A journal article that prints a mantra is not a template for this skill.
