# Offline Brief

## Local Facts

- Purpose: ordinary messages, reminders, and review for Bahasa Karo.
- Catalog: ISO 639-3 `btx`, glottocode `bata1293`. Glottolog name: Batak Karo.
- Peta Bahasa files the same speech as dialek Karo of Bahasa Batak, beside Toba, Mandailing, Simalungun, and Pakpak.
- This skill treats cakap Karo as its own language. It does not generate Toba, Simalungun, or Pakpak.
- Peta Bahasa places Karo in Kabupaten Karo and in named villages in Langkat, Deli Serdang, Dairi, and Simalungun, and in Kota Medan.
- Phrase source: Kamus Bahasa Karo-Indonesia, Pusat Bahasa / Balai Pustaka, 2001, via a public OCR. Level 2.
- Kata Tugas Bahasa Karo (1999) was not opened. Do not add particles from memory of that title.
- The OCR mixes neighboring entries. Only the glosses in `local-mirror.md` are safe to repeat.
- This file is the offline substitute. It does not replace the dictionary.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent vocabulary, merga formulas, or ritual lines.
- Do not open with Horas or close with Mauliate.
- Do not replace `mejuah-juah` with Pakpak `njuah-njuah`.
- Do not swap `ula`, `ola`, and `ulang`. All three show up near a jangan gloss. Quote only the printed phrase.
- Keep schedules, money, health advice, and official instructions in Indonesian.
- If a full sentence is not readable in the source, write the short attested phrase and leave the rest in Indonesian. Label that text mixed.
- Do not repair a broken OCR line into a cleaner Karo sentence.

## Sample Prompts

- "Use $tutur-karo to adapt this short chat for a cousin in Kabanjahe."
- "Review this text. It says Horas and Mauliate. Is that cakap Karo?"
- "Write a neighbor reminder. Mejuah-juah once, rest in Indonesian."
- "Close a group note with bujur. Do not add a merga greeting."

## Safe Output Patterns

- Open with `mejuah-juah` or, for a group, `mejuah-juah kita kerina`.
- Close with `bujur` when thanks is actually needed.
- Name the language `cakap Karo` if the note is about the language itself.
- For an unknown word, keep the Indonesian word.
- For adat, ask for the wording used by the family. Do not compose it.
