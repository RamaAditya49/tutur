# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Karo. Dictionary name in the 2001 book: Bahasa Karo. Glottolog name: Batak Karo. Speakers in the dictionary example say `cakap Karo`.
- ISO 639-3: `btx`. Glottocode: `bata1293`.
- Peta Bahasa: dialek Karo inside the Bahasa Batak map for Sumatra Utara. That map also lists Toba, Mandailing, Simalungun, and Pakpak (Dairi). The page that was read gives difference figures for Toba against Simalungun (69,25 percent), Mandailing (71,25 percent), and Pakpak (75,25 percent). It does not print a Toba–Karo figure. Do not invent one.
- Phrase source: Kamus Bahasa Karo-Indonesia, Siregar, Sukapiring, Tarigan, Sembiring, and Zulkifli, Pusat Bahasa / Balai Pustaka, 2001. Read through the OCR at the adoc.pub link in `sources.md`.
- Source confidence: Level 2.
- Kata Tugas Bahasa Karo (1999) is a real grammar booklet. Its examples are not in this mirror.

Source mirror notes:

- Karo is not Toba. Horas and mauliate are not in the glosses quoted here.
- Karo is not Pakpak. The Pakpak salam in the USU Press book is `njuah-njuah`.
- Karo is not Simalungun. The Simalungun dictionary has its own `horas` entry. Do not copy it into cakap Karo.

## Local Usability

Use this mirror for a chat, a short reminder, or a review. One Karo phrase, then Indonesian.

`mejuah-juah kita kerina` is the group line printed under the headword. One person can answer with `mejuah-juah`. That reply pattern is described on the Indonesian Wikipedia page for the salam. The dictionary itself is the authority for the gloss sehat-sehat / selamat. Do not add a cosmological explanation. The Wikipedia page adds one, and this skill does not repeat it.

Kinship and merga stay in Indonesian. The OCR did not yield a clean set of address terms safe to quote.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Short notes. Only where the Indonesian gloss was still readable.

- `mejuah-juah`: sehat-sehat; selamat.
- `mejuah-juah kita kerina`: the dictionary's dashed example under that headword, glossed sehat kita semua.
- `bujur`: terima kasih. The example sentence in the OCR collides with the next entry, so this skill quotes the gloss and not a rebuilt sentence.
- `ia cakap Karo`: glossed ia berbicara Karo. Use `cakap Karo` as the name of the language in that example.
- `ula ngandung`: the visible words under the menimang example, glossed jangan menangis. The fuller visible string is `ken anakmu e gelah ula ngandung`, glossed timang anakmu itu supaya jangan menangis. The headword spelling is messy in the OCR, so do not add a verb in front of this string.
- `ulang`: a separate headword glossed jangan. Do not drop it into the `ula ngandung` line.
- `ola ko` and `ola kita`: jangan engkau / jangan kita, in entries whose example sentences are too messy or too specific to reuse as templates.
- `aku`: headword glossed saya. No example sentence is quoted, because the neighboring lines are scrambled.

## Generation Rules

- Open with `mejuah-juah` or `mejuah-juah kita kerina`. Close with `bujur` only when thanks is part of the message.
- Do not write Horas, Mauliate, Amang, Inang, or Tulang.
- Do not write Pakpak `njuah-njuah` here.
- Do not invent a full Karo sentence around `aku`.
- Do not recombine `ula` and `ulang`.
- Label mixed text as mixed.
- Ritual text, including muncang kuta, stays with a practitioner. Do not quote a mantra from a journal article into a chat.
