# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Sumbawa. Local name in the sources: Basa Samawa, Semawa, Samawa.
- ISO 639-3: `smw`. Glottocode: `sumb1241`. Glottolog lists it under Bali-Sasak-Sumbawa, then Sasak-Sumbawa, and marks it vigorous.
- Region: western Pulau Sumbawa, from around Plampang to Tongo, Nusa Tenggara Barat.
- Dialects in Peta Bahasa: (1) Sumbawa Besar, called the standard; (2) Taliwang; (3) Jereweh; (4) Tongo. The kamus preface also labels Sumbawa Besar, Taliwang, Jereweh, and Tongo, plus kasar and hormat.
- Separated by Peta Bahasa from Bahasa Sasak and Bahasa Bima.
- Source confidence: Level 2. Kamus Samawa-Indonesia (edisi 2 on the UNRAM PDF; a third edition, 2022, is catalogued by Perpustakaan Kemendikdasmen) and two published honorific studies from Universitas Samawa.
- Transcripts used here are Sumbawa Besar campus speech, not a general dialect survey.
- A 2011 personal lesson page lists extra chat lines such as Rungan balong si. That page is support only and is not used as a generation source in the examples.

Source links are in `sources.md`. This file does not copy the dictionary.

## Local Usability

Use this file offline. Ordinary chat may open with the attested kabar line and then switch to Indonesian. The student transcripts already mix Indonesian words into Samawa. Copy that mix only when the whole line is printed in the source. Do not invent a new mix and call it fluent Samawa.

Sasak is the Lombok language. Bima, often called Mbojo in NTB office names, is the eastern Sumbawa language. A line that "sounds NTB" can be any of the three.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Verified items only. Glosses follow the cited source.

From Ifansyah and Aini, seminar paper, Universitas Samawa, Sumbawa Besar:

- kaji: 'saya', used with respect, often to a lecturer or an older student. saya is also used, including by educated speakers, and the two can alternate.
- kajulen: 'saya', higher respect. The student paper says it was no longer common in ordinary student speech. The later honorific article glosses it as kaji 'saya' plus ulen 'hamba'.
- diri nan: 'anda' for a peer.
- sia: 'anda' for someone older, higher, or a lecturer.
- diri: 'beliau'. diri ta, diri to, and diri ana point nearer or farther.
- kanti: 'sahabat', used as a general address, not only for an old friend.
- sanak salaki: 'saudara laki-laki'. sanak sawai: 'saudara perempuan'.
- papen: 'kakek/nenek' in the same paper's list of general address terms.
- Attested line: "Apa rungan, Kanti?" glossed "Apa kabar, Sahabat?"
- Attested line: "Me pang kaji dapat buku nan?" glossed "Di mana saya dapatkan buku itu?"
- Attested line: "Sia beang ko saya mo." glossed "Anda berikan ke saya saja."
- Attested line: "Diri nan baeng deta ke?" glossed "Anda yang punya ini?"

From the honorific article in Bahastra (same authors, Sumbawa Besar informants):

- kelam: 'anda', singular, higher respect than sia.
- sareasia or sia sarea: 'anda semua'.
- dirinan: peer second person, described as diri plus nan 'itu'.
- Nobility address recorded there: dea, lalu, lala, daeng. Use only if the user supplies the person's real title.

From Kamus Samawa-Indonesia, edisi 2 PDF, lemma rungan:

- rungan: 'berita; kabar'. The entry's example is the frame apa rungan ... with the gloss 'apa kabar kampung halamanmu'.

Do not add ritual formulas, royal speeches, or dialect synonyms that the kamus marks for another dialek.

## Generation Rules

1. Write the meaning in plain Indonesian first.
2. Add only Samawa pieces listed above or supplied by the user with a source.
3. If a full sentence is not in the transcript or the dictionary example, keep it in Indonesian and label the message mixed.
4. Say Sumbawa Besar when using the lines above.
5. Do not aim sia, kelam, or diri nan at the wrong person.
6. Do not borrow Sasak (for example tabeq or matur tampiasih) or Bima wording.
7. For public or professional text, keep time, place, price, and policy in Indonesian.
