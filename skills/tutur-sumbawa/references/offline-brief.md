# Offline Brief

## Local Facts

- Purpose: ordinary messages, short greetings, and review for Bahasa Sumbawa (Basa Samawa, Semawa).
- Region: western Pulau Sumbawa, Kabupaten Sumbawa and Kabupaten Sumbawa Barat, Nusa Tenggara Barat.
- Peta Bahasa: four dialects. Sumbawa Besar is the standard and the majority dialect, used in the old kabupaten center, mass media, and traditional arts. Taliwang, Jereweh, and Tongo are the other three. Difference among them is given as about 65–80%.
- Peta Bahasa separates Sumbawa from Sasak and from Bima, with a difference of about 81–100%.
- ISO 639-3: `smw`. Glottocode: `sumb1241`.
- Source confidence: Level 2. Kamus Samawa-Indonesia exists, and published Sumbawa Besar transcripts supply short lines.
- Speech habit: pick the pronoun before the sentence. kaji, saya, and kajulen are first person. diri nan is a peer "anda". sia is "anda" for someone older or higher. kelam is a higher "anda" than sia in the honorific study.
- Worked examples in this skill are Sumbawa Besar. Do not present them as Taliwang, Jereweh, or Tongo.
- Attested lines to reuse are copied in `local-mirror.md`. They are not a license to translate a paragraph.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent vocabulary, titles, or ritual lines.
- Do not treat Sumbawa Besar as the speech of Taliwang, Jereweh, or Tongo.
- Do not write wedding, tokal, or other adat speeches from a dictionary gloss.
- Keep Indonesian when the Samawa word is missing.
- Ask which dialect the reader uses.
- Do not mix Sasak or Bima into a Samawa line.
- Nobility titles in the honorific study (dea, lalu, lala, daeng) are used only when the user already gives that person's title.

- "Open a Sumbawa Besar chat with the attested kabar line."
- "Check whether this is Samawa or Bima with a few local words."

- "Tell me whether sia or diri nan fits a message to an older cousin."

## Safe Output Patterns

- Open with Apa rungan when asking after someone, then give the errand in Indonesian.
- Use kaji, diri nan, and sia only in the roles the transcript describes.
- For review, flag Sasak, Bima, and unlabeled dialect mixing.
- For unknown terms, keep Indonesian and name the gap.
- For public use, ask a Samawa speaker from the target dialect.
- For admin facts, keep time, price, and document names in Indonesian.
- Mark mixed lines as mixed.
