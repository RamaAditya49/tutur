# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Sumbawa, Basa Samawa, Semawa.
- Folder: tutur-sumbawa.
- ISO 639-3 `smw`. Glottocode `sumb1241`.
- Area: western Pulau Sumbawa. This skill's examples are dialek Sumbawa Besar.
- Other dialects named by Peta Bahasa: Taliwang, Jereweh, Tongo. Sumbawa Besar is the standard in that source.
- Neighbors it is confused with: Bahasa Sasak on Lombok, and Bahasa Bima (Mbojo) in eastern Sumbawa.
- Speech habit to name: honorific pronouns. kaji for a respectful "saya", diri nan for a peer, sia for someone older, kelam when the source's higher grade is required.

## Safety Rules

- Do not invent vocabulary, ritual language, or titles.
- Do not let Sumbawa Besar stand for every Samawa speaker.
- Do not write adat, legal, or official text from a greeting line.
- Keep Indonesian when the Samawa form is unknown.
- Ask for a sample when the user wants Taliwang, Jereweh, or Tongo.
- Do not mix those dialects, and do not mix in Sasak or Bima.
- Use dea, lalu, lala, or daeng only when the user gives that title for a real person.

## Practical Use

Use this skill for source-aware Samawa openings, pronoun choice, and review of machine text. It fits chat and short community notes in Sumbawa Besar. It does not fit a Samawa-only announcement.

## Registers

### Everyday

Attested opening from the Sumbawa Besar transcript:

> Apa rungan, Kanti?

That line means "Apa kabar, Sahabat?" The errand after it stays in Indonesian unless the user quotes another attested line.

### Public

Use the kabar opening, then Indonesian for the time and place. Do not address a whole neighborhood as Kanti unless the note is to friends. For a general notice, "Apa rungan" is the piece supported by the dictionary example "apa kabar", and the rest is Indonesian. Mark the notice mixed.

### Professional

Campus transcripts use kaji and Pak plus the office (Pak Dekan, Pak Kaprodi). Copy that pattern only as a description. A new admin request keeps the deadline in Indonesian. One attested line may be quoted if it really fits, such as a peer asking "Diri nan baeng deta ke?" about a lost item.

### Adat

The kamus glosses tokal as the family call to discuss a wedding. Do not write the tokal speech. Nobility and religious titles in the honorific study are labels for people who already hold them, not words to decorate a caption.
