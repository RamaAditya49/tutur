# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a chat or a short reminder, open with Apa rungan and put the errand in Indonesian. If the addressee is a peer, Kanti is attested as sahabat. If the addressee is older, do not invent a new sia-sentence. Keep the request in Indonesian and mention that sia is the older-addressee pronoun.

## Sample Tasks

- Open a Sumbawa Besar chat with Apa rungan, Kanti?
- Review a text that mixes Sasak or Bima into Samawa.
- Choose kaji, diri nan, or sia for a named addressee and explain the choice.
- Write a community reminder that stays mixed.
- Check whether a line is Sumbawa Besar or an unlabeled other dialect.

## Register And Semantics

- Ask the dialect and the relative age before a public line.
- Keep the Indonesian meaning before any Samawa word is added.
- Skip jokes about Tau Samawa, Sasak, or Dou Mbojo, and skip invented adat lines.
- Leave medical, legal, and money terms in Indonesian.
- kanti in the transcript is an address, "Sahabat", not proof that every reader is a close friend.
- saya inside a Samawa transcript line is attested code-mixing. Do not "purify" it into new Samawa.

## Review Checklist

- Does the output use Indonesian grammar with one Samawa word and call the whole line Samawa?
- Is every Samawa word in the local mirror or in a user sample?
- Is the dialect named, and is it Sumbawa Besar for the lines stored here?
- Is the pronoun matched to peer, older, or group?
- Are Sasak and Bima forms absent?
- Are names, dates, and facts unchanged?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first, then the dialect label.
- Review request: name the risk, then a safer line.
- Public text: Samawa greeting plus Indonesian facts, and a speaker-review note.
