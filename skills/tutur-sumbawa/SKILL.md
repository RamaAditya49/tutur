# Tutur Sumbawa

## Overview

Use this skill for Bahasa Sumbawa, also called Basa Samawa or Semawa, on the western part of Pulau Sumbawa. Peta Bahasa treats dialek Sumbawa Besar as the standard and lists Taliwang, Jereweh, and Tongo beside it. The worked examples in this skill are Sumbawa Besar unless a source label says otherwise.

Short greetings and pronouns are usable when a dictionary example or a published transcript prints them. A full Samawa announcement is not. Sasak and Bima are the neighbors Peta Bahasa measures against. They are separate languages.

Use this skill for source-aware Samawa generation and review. It is not a certified translator. Dialect, adat, public, legal, educational, or official text stays with a Samawa speaker when the line is not in the local sources.

## Workflow

1. Identify the task.
   - Translation needs a printed Samawa sample.
   - Style requests keep the Indonesian meaning and add only attested Samawa.
   - Ask the dialect if the user says Sumbawa without saying Sumbawa Besar, Taliwang, Jereweh, or Tongo.

2. Check source confidence.
   - Read `references/sources.md` first.
   - Prefer Kamus Samawa-Indonesia and the Sumbawa Besar honorific transcripts.
   - A personal lesson blog is support only. Do not let it override the dictionary or the transcript.
   - If the kamus marks a form as kasar, hormat, or a non-standard dialect, keep that label.

3. Compose with source discipline.
   - Keep names, places, titles, dates, and facts unchanged.
   - Use verified Samawa words only.
   - If the sentence is not attested, write Indonesian and label the result mixed.

4. Final pass.
   - Name the dialect.
   - Do not claim native-speaker or official authority.
   - Do not answer in Sasak or Bima.

## Output Style

- For a direct request, give the message first.
- For a review, name the risk, then give a safer rewrite.
- For public, adat, legal, school, or government text, ask for a Samawa speaker review.
- Label mixed Samawa-Indonesian text as mixed.
