# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a chat or a short reminder, quote one dictionary line that really matches, then put the errand in Indonesian. "Dohop ikei" is the short help line. Thanks, if it must be the Ngaju example, is the whole tarima kasih sentence about pertolongan.

## Sample Tasks

- Ask someone to help with "dohop ikei", then explain the task in Indonesian.
- Review a text that says bahasa Dayak and mixes several languages.
- Explain ngaju as upstream, separate from the ethnic name Ngaju.
- Write a community reminder that stays mixed.
- Refuse a ritual or healing formula.

## Register And Semantics

- Ask the river area before a public line. Kasongan is not Kuala Kurun.
- Keep the Indonesian meaning before any Ngaju word is added.
- Skip jokes about Dayak groups, and skip invented adat lines.
- Leave medical, legal, and money terms in Indonesian.
- hapahari in the number example means bersaudara. Do not turn it into a new greeting.
- Code-mixing in the printed thanks line is attested. New mixes are labeled mixed and stay mostly Indonesian.

## Review Checklist

- Does the output use Indonesian grammar with one Ngaju word and call the whole line Ngaju?
- Is every Ngaju word in the local mirror or in a user sample?
- Is the line kept away from other Dayak languages?
- Is ngaju the place-word used only for upstream?
- Are names, dates, and facts unchanged?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?
- Is a ritual or healing request left unwritten?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first, then the source note.
- Review request: name the risk, then a safer line.
- Public text: one quoted Ngaju line plus Indonesian facts, and a speaker-review note.
