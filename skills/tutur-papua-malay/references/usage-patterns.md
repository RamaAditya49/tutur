# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

Give the spoken line first when a corpus clause matches. If the user needs a new fact, add it in Indonesian and mark the message mixed. Mention Sarmi when the clause comes from Kluge's recordings.

## Sample Tasks

- Split cooking with the papeda line, then say what time in Indonesian.
- Ask two people to start walking with the `jalang, ayo` line.
- Review a text that uses Ambon `beta` and calls it Melayu Papua.
- Refuse a Dani, Biak, Sentani, or Asmat request.
- Keep a school or office notice in Indonesian.
- Explain `ko` after a name without coining a new attested sentence.

## Register And Semantics

- `ko` after a kin term or name singles out the addressee. It is not a particle to scatter through Indonesian.
- `sa` is I. `kitong` is we in the walking line. Do not replace them with `beta` or `katong`.
- `pace` and `mace` are man and woman in these examples. They are not interchangeable with Ambon address.
- `jangang` belongs to the quoted warning. Do not regularise that quotation.
- Papuan Malay in this skill is one Malay variety. It does not stand in for a family's other language.

## Review Checklist

- Is every Melayu Papua clause printed in Kluge or in the Wacana quotation stored here?
- Did a new personal name get written into a corpus sentence and presented as attested?
- Are Dani, Biak, Sentani, or Asmat words absent?
- Are Ambon pronouns absent?
- Are times, prices, and places still in Indonesian when the corpus line does not contain them?
- Does an official letter stay in Indonesian, matching Kluge's domain note?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: the clause, then the Indonesian detail.
- Review request: name the other language, then a corpus clause only if the meaning matches.
- Public text: Indonesian body. A corpus line appears as a labelled model when the user wants to hear the voice.
