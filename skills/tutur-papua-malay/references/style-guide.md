# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Melayu Papua.
- Also called Papuan Malay. ISO 639-3 `pmy`, glottocode `papu1250`.
- A regional Malay. The only language this skill writes.
- Not Dani, not Biak, not the Sentani language, not Asmat, and not a blend of them.
- Not Melayu Ambon and not Melayu Ternate.
- The attested voice comes from Sarmi conversations: `sa`, `ko` on a name, `pace`, `mace`, `kitong`, `suda`.

## Safety

- Do not invent vocabulary, clan titles, or ritual language.
- Do not describe this Malay as the one language of Papua.
- Do not move a corpus sentence onto a new name and drop the citation.
- Do not use it for a sermon or a government letter. Kluge records those domains as outside ordinary Papuan Malay.
- Keep medical, legal, and money wording in Indonesian.

## Everyday

A kitchen text can be `mama-ade ko masak daging sa biking papeda e?` with any extra plan in Indonesian. Leaving together can be `sa dengang pace ini kitong dua jalang, ayo`. Asking a named daughter to speak can be `Nofela bicara suda!` when the user is quoting that scene or speaking to Nofela.

## Public

Neighbourhood notices stay in Indonesian: time, place, and the rule. The line `Wili ko jangang gara~gara tanta dia itu!` shows how a child was warned in the recording. It is not rewritten with another child's name.

## Professional

Staff chat may quote one corpus clause and then state the decision in Indonesian. Forms, rosters, and official replies are Indonesian. Unofficial speech inside an office is the limit Kluge allows, and even then only with attested wording.

## Adat Boundary

Ceremony, clan address, and ritual speech of Dani, Biak, Sentani, Asmat, or any other people are not produced here. `pace` and `mace` stay the ordinary man and woman words of the Malay examples. They are not converted into adat titles.
