# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Melayu Papua.
- Glottocode: `papu1250`. ISO 639-3: `pmy`.
- Glottolog places it in Vehicular Malay, with Ambonese Malay as a relative but not the same language.
- Kluge describes one northeast-coast variety from about sixteen hours of conversation and narrative in Sarmi. She calls Papuan Malay the easternmost Malay and the local language of wider communication.
- She also writes that it is not officially used for government, education, or religious preaching.
- Peta Bahasa's Jayapura points show where a Malay observation was made. They do not list Dani, Biak, or Asmat as dialects of this Malay.
- The possessive marker discussed in the Wacana review of the grammar is `punya`. This mirror does not add a short `pu` sentence, because the examples below do not need one.

Source links live in `sources.md`. The grammar is not excerpted beyond these clauses.

## Local Usability

Offline, quote a corpus clause when its meaning matches, then continue in Indonesian. Code-mixing is already how the recordings work: names, kin terms, and everyday nouns sit in one utterance. Do not purify them.

Sarmi wording is the attested wording. A user who names Jayapura, Manokwari, or another town still gets these clauses only as Sarmi-attested lines, not as a claim that every coast speaks this way.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

From Kluge's printed examples.

- `sa`: saya, in `sa biking papeda`.
- `ko`: kamu, attached to the person addressed: `mama-ade ko`, `Wili ko`, `mace ko`.
- `mama-ade`: tante, in example (51).
- `mama-ade ko masak daging sa biking papeda e?`: tante masak daging, saya bikin papeda, ya? Record 080921-001-CvNP.0073.
- `Wili ko jangang gara~gara tanta dia itu!`: Wili, jangan mengganggu tanta itu. The book writes the reduplication as `gara~gara` and the prohibitive as `jangang`. Record 081023-001-Cv.0038.
- `mace ko rasa lucu`: address to a woman, glossed as Madam feeling funny, in a reaction to a story. Record 081010-001-Cv.0206. `mace` is woman.
- `Nofela bicara suda!`: Nofela, bicara. A father asking his daughter to speak. Record 080922-001a-CvPh.0805. `suda` marks the push to do it.
- `sa dengang pace ini kitong dua jalang, ayo`: saya dengan pace ini, kita berdua jalan, ayo. Quoted from the corpus in the Wacana review. `pace` is man. `kitong` is we. `dengang` is with.
- `pace dorang dua ini ke atas`: dua pace ini ke atas. `dorang` is the plural pronoun beside the noun.
- `tong`: we, in the same grammar's pronoun tables, alongside `kitong`.
- `dong`: they, plural pair of `dorang`.
- `tra`: negator, visible in Kluge's interlinear as `tra` glossed neg. This skill does not invent a new `tra` sentence.

## Generation Rules

1. Start from the Indonesian meaning.
2. Use a whole corpus clause if it fits the scene.
3. Keep Kluge's spelling: `jangang`, `dengang`, `gara~gara`, `suda`, `papeda`.
4. Do not retarget `Wili ko` or `Nofela` to a new name and call the new line attested. Say the pattern is name plus `ko`, and quote the original.
5. Leave schedules, money, and policy in Indonesian.
6. If the user asks for Dani, Biak, Sentani, or Asmat, stop.
7. Label mixed text as mixed.

## Register Handling

- Everyday: the papeda offer, the walk line, or `bicara suda` when someone is being asked to talk.
- Public: Indonesian for the notice. The Wili warning is to one named child in a recording, not a poster.
- Professional: Kluge's own limit applies. Operational text stays Indonesian. Unofficial staff chat may quote one corpus clause.
- Adat: no ceremony text. `pace` and `mace` are ordinary person words in this Malay, not titles from another language's ritual.
