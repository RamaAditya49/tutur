# Offline Brief

## Local Facts

- Purpose: short messages in Bahasa Melayu Papua only.
- What it is: a regional Malay, a language of wider communication on the coast.
- What it is not: Dani, Biak, the Sentani language, Asmat, or any other non-Malay Papuan language.
- Map label: Peta Bahasa records Malay at Waena, Abepura, and Sentani in Kota Jayapura and calls the variety Melayu Papua in the longer note.
- Grammar sample: Sarmi, northeast coast, in Kluge's recordings. Do not pretend that one town's wording is every town's wording.
- Codes: glottocode `papu1250`, ISO 639-3 `pmy`.
- Pronouns in the grammar examples: `sa` saya, `ko` kamu, `tong` / `kitong` kita, `dong` / `dorang` mereka.
- Address pattern: kin term or name plus `ko`, as in `mama-ade ko` and `Wili ko`.
- Person words: `pace` laki-laki, `mace` perempuan.
- Kluge: not the language of formal government, classroom instruction, or preaching.
- This skill should work without browsing.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent vocabulary or ritual language from any Papuan people.
- Do not call this skill "Bahasa Papua".
- Do not reuse Melayu Ambon lines (`beta`, `ose`, `ale`, `dangke`, `jang buang sampa`).
- Do not build a new sentence by attaching `ko` to a name that was not in the corpus, then treating the result as attested. Describe the pattern and quote the corpus line.
- `jangang` is the negative imperative in the Wili line. Do not modernise it to `jangan` inside that quotation.
- New times, prices, and places stay in Indonesian.
- Label mixed text as mixed.

## Sample Prompts

- "Use $tutur-papua-malay to review this Melayu Papua text."
- "Make a source-aware Melayu Papua note about cooking papeda."
- "Check whether this is Melayu Papua or a Papuan language such as Dani."
- "Keep the meeting time in Indonesian and open with a corpus line only if it fits."
- "Show pace and mace from the grammar, not as a slogan."

## Safe Output Patterns

- Cooking split: `mama-ade ko masak daging sa biking papeda e?`
- Setting off: `sa dengang pace ini kitong dua jalang, ayo`
- Asking someone to speak: `Nofela bicara suda!`
- A public notice that needs new facts: Indonesian, with the corpus line only as a labelled model.
- Adat of a named Papuan people: refuse and name the limit.
