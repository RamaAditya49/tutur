# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a chat or a short reminder, open with Tabe and put the errand in Indonesian. If an Amfoang word is added, name Oenames or Kaesmetan. Do not build "I am healthy" by lining au and leko up into a new sentence.

## Sample Tasks

- Open a Timor note with Tabe and keep the rest in Indonesian.
- Give au, ho, and the two forms of we, labeled by Amfoang subdialect.
- Review a text that uses leko as a thanks reply.
- Show nub and nua as the two Amfoang words for two, and refuse to choose one for Soe.
- Check a line for Tetun or Melayu Kupang.

## Register And Semantics

- Ask the village when the reader is an elder, a church, or a public sign.
- Keep the Indonesian meaning before any Dawan word is added.
- Skip jokes about Atoni, Tetun, or Kupang, and skip invented adat lines.
- Leave medical, legal, and money terms in Indonesian.
- ho is 'you' in the Amfoang table. It is not a polite title for every guest.
- hai and hit/hita are both glossed we. Do not decide inclusive or exclusive from this file.

## Review Checklist

- Does the output use Indonesian grammar with one Dawan word and call the whole line Dawan?
- Is every Dawan word in the local mirror or in a user sample?
- Is the variety named, or marked unstated for Tabe, Hao, and Kaha'?
- Are Oenames and Kaesmetan forms kept apart?
- Are Tetun and Melayu Kupang absent?
- Is leko still 'sehat'?
- Are names, dates, and facts unchanged?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first, then the variety label.
- Review request: name the risk, then a safer line.
- Public text: Tabe plus Indonesian facts, and a speaker-review note.
