# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Ternate.
- Glottocode: `tern1247`. ISO 639-3: `tft`.
- Family in Glottolog: North Halmahera, Northern North Halmahera, Ternatean, Ternate.
- Tidore, for contrast: glottocode `tido1248`, ISO 639-3 `tvo`.
- Watuseke gives free pronouns and the bound prefixes, plus clauses that can stand alone.
- Duwila and Nurfani, in the Kantor Bahasa journal, give field-style sentences and cite Atjo 2008 for `fajaru` and `fangare`.
- Glottolog's endangerment note, citing Hayami-Allen 2001, says younger speakers on Ternate island often shift to North Moluccan Malay. That is why Malay lines must not be quietly substituted for Bahasa Ternate.
- A Melayu Ternate phrase list uses `Kita tara kanal dia` and `ngana tar mau pi di pante`. The negator `tara`/`tar` and the verb `pi` identify those lines as Malay.

Source links live in `sources.md`. No dictionary page is copied.

## Local Usability

Offline, produce only the clauses listed below, then Indonesian. The prefix is part of the verb. Removing it, or moving it onto an Indonesian verb, breaks the pattern the sources describe.

Ask whether the listener is a peer or someone who should be addressed politely before choosing `ngori` or `fangare`/`fajaru`, and before choosing `ngana` or `ngon`.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

From Watuseke unless noted.

- `ngori`: I, not the polite form in Watuseke. Duwila uses it for either gender when the listener is a peer.
- `fangare`: I, male polite. `fajaru`: I, female polite.
- `ngana`: you singular, lower. `ngon`: you singular polite, and also you plural in Watuseke's table.
- `ngom`: we exclusive. `ngone`: we inclusive.
- `una`: he. `mina`: she. `ana`: they.
- Prefixes with those subjects in Watuseke's make-it set: `to-` with `ngori`/`fangare`/`fajaru`, `no-` with `ngana`, `ni-` with `ngon`, `o-` with `una`, `mo-` with `mina`, `mi-` with `ngom`, `fo-` with `ngone`, `i-` with `ana`.
- `fangare ware ua`: saya tidak tahu.
- `ngana ware nage?`: kamu tahu itu? The you-word is the lower one.
- `namo temo`: ayam jantan berkokok.
- `sidika`: biarkan, jangan diurusi. Imperative of `tika`.
- `ngori to-gulaha`: saya membuatnya. The object is general in Watuseke's gloss "I make it".
- Duwila: `Ngori to-tego toma degodego` (saya duduk di tempat duduk).
- Duwila: `Ngom mi-kudiho toma fala` (kami pulang ke rumah).
- Duwila: `Ngana no-gulaha ake guraka` (kamu membuat air jahe).
- Duwila: `Fajaru totagi toma gura` in the introduction (saya, perempuan, pergi ke kebun). The hyphenation differs from her later `to-` examples. Quote the introduction form only with that note.
- `toma`: to or at, inside the sentences above. `fala`: house. `gura`: garden. `raima`: already, in Duwila's `Ngom mi-Hotu raima` (kami sudah tidur).

## Generation Rules

1. Decide peer versus respectful address before any pronoun.
2. Use a whole attested clause. Do not recombine `ngori` with a prefix taken from another person.
3. Do not write Melayu Ternate (`tara`, `tar mau`, `pi`, `torang`, `kita` as I) in this skill.
4. Do not write Tidore.
5. Put new times, prices, and instructions in Indonesian.
6. Label mixed text as mixed.
7. Do not compose kolano or sultanate address.

## Register Handling

- Everyday peer: `Ngori to-tego toma degodego` or `fangare ware ua` when the speaker's gender fits `fangare` for the not-knowing line.
- Respectful: name `fangare` or `fajaru` and keep the rest in Indonesian if no full polite sentence matches.
- Public: Indonesian for the notice. A Ternate clause only if it is one of the lines above and the addressee fits.
- Professional: facts in Indonesian.
- Adat and court: no generated formulas.
