# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Ternate.
- North Halmahera language. ISO 639-3 `tft`, glottocode `tern1247`.
- Not Melayu Ternate, not Melayu Maluku Utara, not bahasa pasar.
- Not Tidore (ISO 639-3 `tvo`, glottocode `tido1248`), even where Peta Bahasa uses a Tidore dialect label on the Ternate page.
- Ordinary speech uses a free pronoun plus a prefixed verb. That pair is the voice of this skill.

## Safety

- Do not invent vocabulary, titles, or kesultanan formulas.
- Do not treat bahasa kolano as a template. The phrase is historical description in Duwila's introduction.
- Do not guess rank. Ask, or stay in Indonesian.
- Do not apply Ternate prefixes to Malay verbs.
- Gender in `fangare` and `fajaru` follows the sources: male speaker and female speaker in the polite first person.

## Everyday

A peer can get `Ngori to-tego toma degodego` or `Ngom mi-kudiho toma fala` when those meanings fit, then Indonesian for the detail. `fangare ware ua` covers "I don't know" in Watuseke's male polite frame. `sidika` is the attested "leave it".

## Public

Notices about time, place, and rules stay in Indonesian. A public line that addresses strangers should not open with `ngana`, because that pronoun is the lower you. If a sourced inclusive or exclusive we-clause fits a work group that already includes the listeners, it can stand as one clause only.

## Professional

Rosters, clinic notes, school letters, and prices stay in Indonesian. Naming `fangare` or `fajaru` as the respectful I is allowed. Building a new respectful sentence is not.

## Adat Boundary

This skill has no attested sultanate, installation, or marriage formula. Do not create one. Household clauses about sitting, going home, or making ginger water are not court language and must not be dressed up as if they were.
