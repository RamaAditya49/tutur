# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Mandar. ISO 639-3 `mdr`. Glottocode `mand1442`.
- Region: Sulawesi Barat, principally Polewali Mandar and Majene, plus the extra Peta Bahasa points noted in `offline-brief.md`.
- Authority for phrases: Kamus Mandar–Indonesia, edisi revisi, 2025.
- Orthography: 19 consonants and 5 vowels in the dictionary front matter. The glottal is written `q`.
- Source confidence: Level 2.
- Full links: `sources.md`.

Source mirror notes:

- The dictionary says free pronouns (seri I) are less usual in polite speech unless the speaker is emphasizing or answering a question. Speakers more often use the inverse series (seri III).
- `basa` is the dictionary's word in phrases such as `basa to Balanipa`, `basa to Banggae`, and `basa to Pamboang`. Those are variety labels, not a single slogan.
- Bugis and Makassar etiquette is not a source for this mirror.

## Local Usability

Use a whole printed example when the meaning matches: eating, going home, coming tomorrow, a piece of news, sit down, do not take that. Then continue in Indonesian. Do not mine the dictionary for loose roots and build a new clause.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Printed examples and glosses:

- `iyau` — saya, aku. `iyau pole dionging` — saya yang datang kemarin.
- `iqo` — kamu. `iqo nasio` — kamu yang disuruhnya.
- `itaq` — anda, bapak, kata ganti orang kedua yang hormat. `itaq na paralluang` — anda yang diperlukan.
- `iyamiq` — kami. `iyamiq naperoa` — kami yang diundang.
- `iya` — ia. `iya moka` — ia tidak mau. This is not a yes-word and not `iye`.
- `tabeq` — permisi, in the `daeng` entry.
- `diang karewa macoa u tarima` — ada kabar baik yang kuterima. `karewa` is glossed kabar on the same stress section.
- `madondong` — besok. `poleo medondong!` — datanglah kamu besok!
- `dionging` — kemarin. `malai mi dionging` — ia sudah pulang kemarin.
- `peqoro moqo!` — duduklah kamu! `meqoro` is duduk in the prefix section.
- `meloaq ummande` — saya mau makan.
- `palaimoqo!` — pulanglah kamu!
- `malaiaq mendiolo` — saya pulang lebih dahulu.
- `daq` — jangan. The printed warning `da muala i doiqna!` is glossed jangan kamu ambil uangnya. Quote that whole warning rather than inventing a new `daq` sentence.
- `u timbe i asunna` — kulempar anjingnya. `uande` — kumakan. These show the `u-` actor prefix for saya. Do not recombine them with new objects.
- `boyang` — rumah, inside `mettama i tau mai di boyang` in the `daeng` entry, glossed as silakan masuk ke rumah. The entry's dash stands for `daeng`. Do not add a new address title.

Person endings visible in the examples, not a free paradigm to extend:

- `-aq` in `meloaq` and `malaiaq` for saya.
- `-o` / `moqo` in `peqoro moqo` and `palaimoqo` for kamu.
- `mu-` in `muitai` "kamu melihat dia".

## Generation Rules

1. Start from the Indonesian meaning.
2. Use a full dictionary example when it matches. Do not glue roots into a new sentence.
3. Keep `q`.
4. Use `iyau` or `iqo` only when the dictionary example already does, or when the user is emphasizing.
5. Use `itaq` only in the printed respectful example, unless the user supplies another `itaq` sentence.
6. Label mixed text as mixed.
7. Do not compose adat verse, including `kalindaqdaq`.

## Register Handling

- Everyday: clitic examples such as `meloaq ummande` and `poleo medondong`.
- Passing someone: `tabeq`, then Indonesian.
- Respectful: `itaq na paralluang` only for "you are the one needed". Otherwise Indonesian Bapak or Ibu.
- Public and professional: Indonesian for the action line.
- Adat: ask for the local text. Do not invent it.
