# Offline Brief

## Local Facts

- Purpose: ordinary Mandar-aware messages and review.
- Region: Sulawesi Barat. Core areas named by Peta Bahasa include Napo (Limboro, Polewali Mandar) and several Majene villages: Labuang, Adolang, Mosso, Ulidang, Mekkatta, Ulumanda.
- Further map points include Pinrang (Lero, Suppa) and a Masalembu (Sumenep) row. Do not treat those as the same village speech.
- Difference from Mamasa and Mamuju on that map: about 81–100 percent.
- ISO 639-3: `mdr`. Glottocode: `mand1442`.
- Dictionary: Kamus Mandar–Indonesia, edisi revisi, 2025. Glottal stop is written `q`.
- Speech habit: person marking prefers the inverse series. `iyau` and `iqo` emphasize. `itaq` is the respectful second person.
- Variety names printed in the dictionary include `basa to Balanipa`, `basa to Banggae`, and `basa to Pamboang`.
- Level 2 for short sourced phrases.
- Not Bugis. Not Makassar. No `iye` in this brief.

## Guardrails

- Do not invent vocabulary.
- Do not respell `q` as an apostrophe.
- Do not replace `tabeq` with `tabe'`.
- Do not add `iye`.
- Do not treat Balanipa, Banggae, and Pamboang as one wording.
- Keep fees, deadlines, clinic advice, and legal lines in Indonesian.
- Label mixed text as mixed.
- Do not write new `kalindaqdaq` or other adat verse.

## Sample Prompts

- "Use $tutur-mandar to answer a cousin who asked if I am coming tomorrow."
- "Review this. It looks like Bugis tabe' pasted onto Indonesian."
- "Write a neighbor note. One Mandar sentence from the dictionary, the rest Indonesian."
- "The addressee is older. Use itaq only in a printed example, not a new sentence."

## Safe Output Patterns

- Coming or eating: `meloaq ummande`, `poleo medondong`, `palaimoqo`.
- News: `diang karewa macoa u tarima`, then the actual news in Indonesian.
- Permission to pass: `tabeq`, then Indonesian.
- Respectful address: quote `itaq na paralluang` only when that meaning fits. Otherwise say Bapak or Ibu in Indonesian.
