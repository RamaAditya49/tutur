# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Bima, Nggahi Mbojo. People: Dou Mbojo in the public descriptions.
- ISO 639-3 `bhp`. Glottocode `bima1247`.
- Place: Kabupaten Bima, Kota Bima, Kabupaten Dompu, east of Sumbawa island. Further speech noted outside that core.
- Not Bahasa Sumbawa (Samawa). Not Bahasa Sasak. Not a dialect of either.

## Safety

- Do not invent vocabulary, patu lines, kande, or jiki.
- Do not mix nggahi halus and nggahi kasa toward one person.
- Do not generate a respectful nickname for a name that is not in Hasan's examples.
- Do not translate the school book's unglossed `Santabe ta la mada...` sentence.
- Keep Indonesian for unlisted words.
- Label mixed text as mixed.

## Everyday

Peer to peer: `nahu` and `nggomi`, opener `Santabe ta` or `Bune haba?`. Younger to older: `mada` and `ita`. The plan of the day stays Indonesian. `Sakola tabe nggomi Hama?` is the published peer question. It is not a pattern for elders.

## Public

A neighborhood note uses `Santabe ta` and Indonesian. Do not address a mixed-age group with `nggomi`. Do not promise `Haba tahopa` as a canned reply unless the user is actually answering "apa kabar" and the support-level gloss is acceptable to them.

## Professional

Office, school, and clinic details stay in Indonesian. Halus pronouns may open a note to an older client (`mada`, `ita`) if a Bima word is wanted at all. Document names, money, and deadlines are not translated.

## Adat Boundary

The curriculum book names `kande` (including kande for a sultan's installation, `tuha ro lanti`), `jiki kapanca`, `rawa Mbojo`, and the literary habit `patu`. Those are boundaries. Do not compose them and do not paste the verses from that book into a new message. Hasan's `lia kai` name changes are also not a generator: only the published pairs Hama/Hima, Hasa/Heso, and Mina/Mene may be repeated, and only when explaining the pattern.
