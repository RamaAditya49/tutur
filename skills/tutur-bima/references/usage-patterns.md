# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a cousin or a neighbor in Bima or Dompu, return the message first. Choose halus or kasa before writing a pronoun. Open with `Santabe ta` or `Bune haba?` when that phrase fits. Keep the plan in Indonesian. Mark mixed text as mixed.

## Sample Tasks

- Reply to a peer who asks kabar. Use `nggomi` / `nahu`, not `ita`.
- Rewrite the same note to an older neighbor. Use `ita` / `mada`.
- Review a text that uses `nggomi` toward a teacher or an elder.
- Write a neighborhood reminder without aiming `nggomi` at the whole group.
- Check a text the user hopes is Bahasa Sumbawa when they asked for Bima. Keep the languages apart. Do not call Samawa a dialect of Mbojo.
- Review a `patu` or `kande` request without adding lines.

## Register And Semantics

- Halus and kasa are a system, not seasoning. One addressee gets one set.
- `Sakola tabe nggomi Hama?` is a peer question in Hasan's example. Do not aim `nggomi` plus a casual nickname at an elder.
- `lia kai` nicknames are not generated for new names.
- Father terms in Hasan's list are alternatives from the field, not a stack of titles.
- Samawa and Sasak stay out even if the user says "bahasa Sumbawa bagian timur". East Sumbawa in Peta Bahasa is Bima. The Sumbawa language is the other one.

## Review Checklist

- Is the pronoun set either halus (`mada`, `ita`) or kasa (`nahu`, `nggomi`), matching who speaks?
- Is `Santabe ta` or `Bune haba?` still a short phrase, with the support note if it is the only Bima in the line?
- Did a new personal name get a vowel swap that no source records?
- Is any Samawa or Sasak wording present?
- Are `patu`, `kande`, and `jiki` left uncomposed?
- Are dates, prices, and places unchanged?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first, and name the register in a short note.
- Review request: mark the pronoun error, then rewrite.
- Public text: `Santabe ta`, Indonesian facts, no kasa pronoun for a mixed crowd.
