# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language/register: Bahasa Bajo (Peta Bahasa), Flores Sea spelling.
- Self-name / outsider name / language name, Nuraini: Sama / Bajo / Baun Same.
- Glottolog: Indonesian Bajau `indo1317`. ISO 639-3: `bdl`.
- Not Sabah: West Coast Bajau `west2560`, ISO `bdr`.
- Peta Bahasa kode 060. Separate rows exist for Bajau Pondong, Bajau Semayap, Bajau Tungkal Satu, and Bajo Delang.
- Nuraini 2010 field anchor: Sapeken, Kangean, Sumenep, plus the Flores Sea shores she names.
- JIPA 2019/2021: one speaker from Tanjung Luar, East Lombok. Same ISO, q-spelling.
- Grammar on record, not quoted: Candrawati et al., Struktur Bahasa Bajo, Pusat Bahasa, 1997.

## Local Usability

Use this offline for single glossed words. A chat that needs a full Bajo clause should stay Indonesian after the word. Pondong (Derawan, Penajam, Pondang Baru) is a different Peta Bahasa language with its own three dialects. Do not answer it from Sapeken words.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Nuraini 2010, Flores Sea. Apostrophe marks a glottal stop in her transcription:

- `ma'i` — come here; mari.
- `nggai` — no, not. Often two syllables in slower speech.
- `malasso` — good, well. Rapid speech may sound malaso; she says speakers still insist on the long form when asked.
- `nginta` — to eat.
- `kampoh` — village, hamlet.
- `ba'u` — new.
- `aha` — person. Sabah cognate in her comparison is `a'a`, not for use here.
- `nganjame` — to work.
- `ellau` — day.
- `boé` — water.
- `lelle` — man, male.

JIPA East Lombok, do not paste into a Nuraini line:

- `kampoh` village (same spelling).
- `pasaq` come, enter.
- `nyapah` to have breakfast.
- `cabiiq` chili.
- `bebeq` duck.
- `aahaaq` people, someone.
- `aahaq` Sunday.
- `baseq` wet.

`pasaq` is not a substitute sentence for `ma'i`. They are different glosses in different write-ups.

## Generation Rules

1. Default label: Bahasa Bajo, Peta Bahasa, Flores Sea / Sapeken words.
2. If the user names Pondong, Semayap, Tungkal Satu, Delang, Nebe-only, Kaung Bajo Tano, or Sabah, stop and ask for a sample from that place.
3. Insert one glossed word. Do not conjugate it.
4. Write the practical point in Indonesian.
5. Do not invent a ceremony, a title, or a "traditional Bajo" blessing.
6. Do not mark the result as official or native-verified.
