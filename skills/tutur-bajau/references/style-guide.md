# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Public name in this skill: Bahasa Bajo, the Peta Bahasa entry. Bajau is the other name outsiders use. Sama is the self-name Nuraini records. Baun Same is the language name in that article.
- Default words: Flores Sea, Sapeken side of the Peta Bahasa map.
- Glottocode / ISO: `indo1317` / `bdl`.
- Outside: Bajau Pondong, Bajau Semayap, Bajau Tungkal Satu, Bajo Delang, Kaung Bajo Tano unless separately sourced, West Coast Bajau `bdr`.

## Safety

- Do not invent vocabulary or a shared ceremonial style.
- Do not treat nine Sama-Bajau languages in Pallesen's count, which Nuraini cites, as one speakable dialect.
- Do not build verbs with ng- or ma- beyond the words she already glosses (`nginta`, `nganjame`, `malasso`).
- East Lombok q-spelling and Flores Sea apostrophes stay in separate notes.

## Everyday

Call someone with `ma'i`, refuse with `nggai`, call a village `kampoh`, call a new thing `ba'u`. Then talk Indonesian. That mix is the whole everyday register this skill can support. It is shore speech in the sources, not a costume of sea-nomad phrases.

## Public

A notice to many villages cannot be one Bajo sentence, because the map itself splits shores and dialects. Write the notice in Indonesian. A single word such as `nggai` may mark "no" if the cancellation is the point.

## Professional

Clocks, queues, and forms stay Indonesian. `ba'u` can sit beside "jadwal baru" as the gloss, not as a letter. There is no attested Bajo admin formula here.

## Adat Boundary

No wedding speech, boat launching, or funeral line was taken from Nuraini, the JIPA sketch, or the unread 1997 grammar. Do not compose one "for all Bajau". A rite at Derawan is Bajau Pondong business. A rite at Labuan Lalar is Kaung Bajo Tano business. Ask a speaker from that shore.
