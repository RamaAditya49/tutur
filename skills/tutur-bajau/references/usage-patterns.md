# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

Return the message first. A Bajo word from Nuraini stands only as that gloss: `ma'i` is "come here", `nggai` is "no", `kampoh` is "village". The sentence around it is Indonesian. Say mixed. Say the spelling is Flores Sea / Sapeken, inside the wider Peta Bahasa entry Bahasa Bajo, not a single village dialect and not Bajau Pondong.

## Sample Tasks

- Chat: `ma'i`, then Indonesian for where to meet.
- Public reminder: `nggai` if the event is off, then the new time in Indonesian.
- Admin: `ba'u` only to mark that a schedule is the new one. The hours stay Indonesian.
- Adat: no rite is on file. Refuse a pan-Bajau ceremony.
- Review: flag Sabah sentences, Pondong claims, and q-spelling mixed into apostrophe words.

## Review Checklist

- Is the target Bahasa Bajo (Peta Bahasa), and is the word from the Flores Sea list?
- Was Pondong, Semayap, Tungkal Satu, Delang, or Sabah wrongly folded in?
- Is every Bajo item a glossed word, not a new clause?
- Are `ma'i` and `pasaq` kept in their own sources?
- Is Baun Same used as the language name Nuraini records, not as a slogan?
- Are dates and places still Indonesian?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?
- If the shore was not named, does the reply still say Flores Sea / Sapeken rather than "all Bajau"?
