# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Gorontalo. Alternate name in Glottolog's multitree list and in the conversation book: Hulontalo.
- ISO 639-3: `gor`. Glottocode: `goro1259`. Glottolog status comment: threatened, 6b.
- Classification: Austronesian, Malayo-Polynesian, Greater Central Philippine, Gorontalo-Mongondow, Gorontalic.
- Not Melayu Manado. Not Minahasa. Peta Bahasa records a Minahasa (Toulour Jaton) community at Reksonegoro, Tibawa, as a different language inside the province.
- Region: Bone Bolango, Gorontalo Utara, Kota Gorontalo. Bolango dialect also in Bolaang Uki, Bolaang Mongondow Selatan, Sulawesi Utara.
- Peta Bahasa dialects: Suwawa, Atinggola, Kota, Bolango. Differences about 51%–78%.
- Glottolog dialects, a separate list: East Gorontalo `east2479`, Gorontalo Kota `goro1260`, Limboto `limb1269`, Tilamuta `tila1238`, West Gorontalo `west2556`. Do not fuse this list with Suwawa/Atinggola/Bolango.
- Dictionary: Pateda, Kamus Bahasa Gorontalo-Indonesia, 1977, and the 2001 Balai Pustaka edition. Not copied here.
- Teaching book: Mohamad Karmin Baruadi, Percakapan Bahasa Gorontalo, Universitas Negeri Gorontalo repository.

## Local Usability

This file is the offline substitute for browsing. Greetings and thanks come from the conversation book's parallel list, so the Indonesian gloss is the book's own. Pronouns `wau` and `yio` come from Pateda 1977 examples. `Temei` and `Tilei` come from a secondary report of the dictionary and stay marked as such.

Ordinary chat may use those greetings. Adat speech and a full Gorontalo announcement need a speaker. Kota dialect is one variety, not the whole language.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

From Baruadi's conversation book, phrase then the book's Indonesian:

- `Wolo habali?` and `Wololo habali?`: apa kabar? / bagaimana kabar?
- `Woloolo habali olanto?`: bagaimana kabar Anda? The same book also prints `Wololo habaliinto?` and `wololo habali?` in sample dialogues. Keep the printed form. Do not add another variant.
- `Paladu u dumodupo`: selamat pagi. Polite/parallel line `Paladu olanto u dumodupo`.
- `Paladu u mohuloonu`: selamat siang. `Paladu u lolaango`: selamat sore. `Paladu u hui`: selamat malam. Each also has an `olanto` parallel in the list.
- `Odu'olo`: terima kasih. Also printed `odu'ola`. `Odu'olo po'odaata`: terima kasih banyak.
- `Piyohu, odu'olo`: baik, terima kasih.
- `Oo jo, odu'olo`: o iya, terima kasih.
- `Watia odu'ola ode olanto`: saya mengucapkan terima kasih pada Anda.
- `Bo oditopo Pa'a Haji, watia mohintupo`: baiklah pak Haji, saya permisi dulu. This is a full glossed line in a sample dialogue. Do not swap the name or the office.

From Pateda 1977, not from the conversation book:

- `wau`: aku. `yio`: engkau.
- Example `yio potibuloa te: to`: engkau duduklah di situ.
- Example `wau mao mohama buku`: aku pergi mengambil buku.
- These are grammar examples. Do not recombine them into a new errand.

Support, Gorut News citing Pateda:

- `Temei` plus a nephew's name: married man. `Tilei` plus a nephew's name: married woman.
- Their quoted line: `Temei Dula wolilei Dula mai hamaa lemei Asi wolilei Asi to Hulondalo`, glossed "Pak Dula dan Bu Dula akan datang dijemput Pak Asi dan Bu Asi di Gorontalo." Quote it only as that example. Do not change the names.

`to Hulondalo` in that line is the place Gorontalo. The conversation book writes `Hulontalo` for the same place in `Ichsan lo Hulontalo` and `SMANSA Hulontalo`. Both spellings are printed. Do not invent a third.

## Generation Rules

1. Write the meaning in plain Indonesian first.
2. Open with one attested greeting: `Wolo habali?`, `Woloolo habali olanto?`, or a `Paladu` line that matches the time of day.
3. Close thanks with `Odu'olo` if thanks is needed.
4. Leave every unlisted content word in Indonesian.
5. Do not import Manado Malay frames.
6. Do not merge Peta Bahasa dialects with Glottolog dialects.
7. Mark mixed text as mixed.
8. For adat or a full speech, ask for a speaker sample.

## Starter Lexicon Policy

Add a Gorontalo word only when a teaching-book gloss or a Pateda example gives the meaning. Do not copy either book. Do not treat the Kantor Bahasa picture-dictionary announcement as a word list. It describes an app. It does not supply entries to this skill.

## Register Handling

- Everyday cousin: `Wolo habali?` then Indonesian.
- Polite guest or elder: `Woloolo habali olanto?` or `Paladu olanto` plus the time-of-day phrase.
- Public: one `Paladu` line, facts in Indonesian, `Odu'olo` at the end if thanks is real.
- Professional: Indonesian for documents and deadlines, `Odu'olo` only as thanks.
- Adat: no composed ritual. `Temei` / `Tilei` is an address habit reported from the dictionary, not a wedding script.
