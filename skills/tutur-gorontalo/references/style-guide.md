# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Gorontalo. Also Hulontalo in the conversation book and in Glottolog's alternate-name list.
- ISO 639-3 `gor`. Glottocode `goro1259`.
- Place: Bone Bolango, Gorontalo Utara, Kota Gorontalo, and Bolango speech in Bolaang Mongondow Selatan.
- Not Melayu Manado. Not Minahasa. Not Gorontalo Malay small talk built from `sto` and `kong`.

## Safety

- Do not invent vocabulary or adat formulas.
- Do not merge the Peta Bahasa dialect list with the Glottolog dialect list.
- Do not normalize the conversation book's spelling variants into a new one.
- Do not coin a `Temei` or `Tilei` teknonym.
- Keep Indonesian for unattested words.
- Label mixed text as mixed.

## Everyday

Cousin chat opens with `Wolo habali?`. Thanks closes with `Odu'olo`. `wau` and `yio` stay the Pateda pronouns aku and engkau. Plans, prices, and meeting points stay Indonesian.

The book's `Oo jo, odu'olo` is one glossed thanks line. It is not a switch into Melayu Manado.

## Public

Match the time of day to the book's `Paladu` line: `Paladu u dumodupo` pagi, `Paladu u mohuloonu` siang, `Paladu u lolaango` sore, `Paladu u hui` malam. Use the `olanto` parallel when the crowd is being addressed as Anda. The instruction under that greeting is Indonesian.

## Professional

Letters, school notes, and office chats keep document names, hours, and money in Indonesian. `Odu'olo` is thanks, not a letterhead. `Pa'a` appears in the sample `Pa'a Haji` and `Pa'a Guru` beside the Indonesian "pak". Do not invent other titles on that pattern.

## Adat Boundary

Marriage address `Temei` / `Tilei` plus a nephew's name is a reported dictionary habit, support-level, not a wedding speech. Do not compose ritual formulas, religious set pieces, or adat procedure. If the user pastes a line, check it against the conversation book and the Pateda pronouns, and mark the rest unchecked.
