# Usage Patterns

## Ordinary Output

For a chat, a caption, or a short reminder, open with an attested Tombulu greeting and put the errand in Indonesian. Keep the answer short. Say mixed when both languages are in one message.

## Sample Tasks

- Rewrite a short Indonesian chat so it opens in Tombulu.
- Review a text that claims to be Tombulu and is mostly Melayu Manado.
- Make two short forms: a morning hello, and a how-are-you line, each copied from the phrase table.
- Localize a neighbor reminder without translating the schedule.
- Check spelling of le'os / leos / le?os against the grammar and the phrase table.

## Register And Semantics

- Ask the village when the reader is an elder, a church, or a public sign.
- Keep the Indonesian meaning before any local word is added.
- Skip jokes about Minahasa groups and skip invented adat lines.
- Leave medical, legal, and money terms in Indonesian.
- tou means 'orang' in the grammar. It is not a title to invent for guests.
- Niko is the attested 'engkau'. Do not invent a polite second person.

## Review Checklist

- Does the output use Indonesian grammar with one Tombulu word and call the whole line Tombulu?
- Is every Tombulu word in the local mirror or in a user sample?
- Is the language Tombulu, not Tonsea, Tontemboan, Toulour, or Melayu Manado?
- Are names, dates, and facts unchanged?
- Is mixed text labeled mixed?
- Does public text keep the time and place unambiguous in Indonesian?
- Is the l-pronunciation note kept as a note, not turned into new spellings?

## Output Patterns

- Direct request: message first, then one source note if a Tombulu word was used.
- Review request: name the risk, then a safer line.
- Public text: Tombulu greeting plus Indonesian facts, and a speaker-review note.
