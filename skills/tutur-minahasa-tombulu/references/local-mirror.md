# Local Mirror

## Source Mirror

- Language: Bahasa Tombulu. Alternate catalog names include Minahasa, Tombalu, Tombula, and Toumbulu. Use Tombulu unless the user asks for a catalog alias.
- ISO 639-3: `tom`. Glottocode: `tomb1243`.
- Region: northern Sulawesi. Grammars and the phrase table place speakers in Tomohon and in Tombulu, Tombariri, Mandolang, Pineleng, and parts of Sonder. Peta Bahasa's mapped Tombulu points are Lemoh and Woloan Dua.
- Peta Bahasa frame: Bahasa Minahasa has dialects Tountemboan, Toulour Jaton, and Tombulu. Difference among those dialects is given as about 68–77%. Tonsea and Tonsawang are the comparison languages, not Tombulu dialects in that entry.
- Glottolog frame: Tombulu is its own spoken L1 language, Austronesian, status shifting.
- Source confidence: Level 2. Anchors are Struktur Bahasa Tombulu (Sahulata, Yahya, Moningkey, Nanlohy, 1993) and Fonologi Bahasa Tombulu (Makalew-Palar, Kembuan, Terok, 1994).
- Phrase support: English Wikipedia Tombulu phrase and vocabulary tables. Do not copy the prayer texts stored on that page.
- Orthography note: Pedoman Membaca dan Menulis Bahasa Tombulu (UKIT Tomohon, 2004) says a writing standard was not yet official. e and é are distinguished there, and z is used for a retroflex sound that older names still spell with r (Rumengkor, Tatawiran).

Source links are listed in `sources.md`. This file does not copy a dictionary.

## Local Usability

Use this file offline to decide what can be said in Tombulu and what stays in Indonesian. Ordinary chat may open with an attested greeting. Schedules, money, medicine, and official instructions stay in Indonesian.

The practical speech habit is a short Tombulu opening, then Indonesian for the errand. That mix is normal in town. It is not a full Tombulu translation.

Melayu Manado is the usual town language around Manado and is a different language. Tonsea, Tontemboan, and Toulour are the neighbors people swap in by mistake.

## Vocabulary And Semantics

Verified items only. Glosses follow the cited source. Spelling is copied, not normalized.

- Syambae: "good morning" in the Wikipedia phrase/vocabulary table.
- Kura-mo? leos 'man?: "How are you? all good?" in that same phrase table.
- Niko: "you" in the Wikipedia table; Struktur Bahasa Tombulu glosses niko / nikó? as 'engkau'.
- Makapulu Leos: "thank you" in the Wikipedia phrase table. Do not expand it.
- le?os: 'baik' in Struktur Bahasa Tombulu. The phrase table writes leos and Le'os. These are spelling variants, not three new words.
- tou: 'orang' in the 1993 grammar. The 2004 guide writes tou Sawangan 'orang Sawangan'.
- wale: 'rumah' in the 1993 grammar. ma- + wale is described as makawale 'mempunyai rumah'.
- kuman: 'makan' in the 1993 grammar.
- lewo?: 'jahat' in the 1993 grammar. Wikipedia lists Lewo 'bad'.
- kulo: 'white' in the Wikipedia pronunciation note, used there to illustrate Tombulu l. Do not build new l-words from this note.

Do not add kinship titles, church formulas, or place-name etymologies that are not in these sources.

## Generation Rules

1. Write the meaning in plain Indonesian first.
2. Add only Tombulu pieces listed above or supplied by the user with a source.
3. If a full sentence is not attested, keep the sentence in Indonesian and label the message mixed.
4. Do not conjugate or add affixes beyond the grammar examples already cited.
5. Do not replace Melayu Manado particles (jo, no, sto) with a claim that the line is Tombulu.
6. For public or professional text, keep time, place, price, and policy in Indonesian.
7. When the user asks for Tonsea, Tontemboan, or Toulour, stop and say this skill is Tombulu only.
