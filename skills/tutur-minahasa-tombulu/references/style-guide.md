# Style Guide

## Language Identity

- Name: Bahasa Tombulu.
- Folder: tutur-minahasa-tombulu.
- ISO 639-3 `tom`. Glottocode `tomb1243`.
- Area: Tomohon and the Tombulu-speaking villages of Minahasa, Sulawesi Utara.
- Peta Bahasa records Tombulu as a dialect of Bahasa Minahasa at Lemoh and Woloan Dua, next to Tountemboan and Toulour.
- Neighbors it is confused with: Melayu Manado in town speech; Tonsea; Tontemboan / Tountemboan; Toulour.
- Speech habit to name: a short greeting, then Indonesian. Sources also single out Tombulu l as unlike the l of the other Minahasan languages.

## Safety Rules

- Do not invent vocabulary, ritual language, or titles.
- Do not let one village stand for every Tombulu speaker.
- Do not write sacred, adat, legal, or official text from a greeting list.
- Keep Indonesian when the Tombulu form is unknown.
- Ask for a sample when the user wants a village voice.
- Do not mix Tombulu with Tonsea, Tontemboan, or Toulour.
- Do not normalize le?os, le'os, and leos into one new spelling.

## Practical Use

Use this skill for source-aware Tombulu openings, tone checks, and review of machine text. It fits chat and short community notes. It does not fit a Tombulu-only announcement.

## Registers

### Everyday

Attested openings:

- Syambae.
- Kura-mo? leos 'man?

Reply to the errand in Indonesian. Niko is the attested word for engkau. le?os is the grammar spelling of 'baik'.

### Public

Use one attested greeting, then Indonesian for the time and place.

> Syambae. Kerja bakti besok jam 7 di balai. Bawa sapu sendiri.

That line is mixed. Review it before it is printed or read on a loudspeaker.

### Professional

For school, clinic, village office, or guest-house admin, keep the deadline and the document name in Indonesian. A Tombulu hello can sit on the first line only.

### Adat

Do not compose adat speech, Malesung text, or church prayers. The public phrase table includes religious lines. Leave them there. Ask for the text the family or the church already uses.
