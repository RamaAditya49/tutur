# Language Skills

This document gives agentic tools a compact index of Tutur's current language and register coverage. The detailed working material remains inside each skill's `references/` folder.

## Register Skills

| Skill | Use for | Local context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `tutur-humanizer` | Natural Indonesian rewriting across formal, professional, casual, gaul, and sales-adjacent contexts. | AI-phrase scanner, Indonesian writing patterns, KBBI/EYD/PUPI references. |
| `tutur-korporat-profesi` | Sales, CS, admin, HRD, finance, marketing, leadership, internal chat, and WhatsApp business. | Role map, channel guidance, corporate code-mix rules, contextual examples. |
| `tutur-jabodetabek-urban` | Jaksel, Jaktim/Jakarta casual, Bekasi, and neutral Jabodetabek urban styles. | Code-mix guardrails, anti-stereotype notes, local style examples. |

## Regional Language Skills

| Skill | Everyday | Formal/public | Adat/ceremony | Key caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tutur-aceh` | Short ordinary Acehnese phrases when covered by KBDA/source notes. | Use restrained source-backed wording. | Review required for ritual, religious, legal, or adat formulas. | Do not apply address terms such as `abu` or `ado` generically. |
| `tutur-gayo` | Short Gayo vocabulary-backed messages. | Use Indonesian baseline plus verified Gayo phrases. | Review required for community/adat wording. | Ask target area when the Gayo community context matters. |
| `tutur-minangkabau` | Everyday Minang community copy. | Public copy can use simple reviewed patterns. | Review required for adat, title, kinship, and ceremonial text. | Do not improvise adat formulas. |
| `tutur-sunda` | Loma/everyday or polite variants when level is known. | Use polite/public wording for schools, institutions, and elder-facing texts. | Review required for ceremonial/local dialect use. | Speech-level consistency matters more than isolated word choice. |
| `tutur-jawa` | Ngoko/everyday or krama variants when level is known. | Use krama/public style for elder-facing or formal use. | Review required for court, ritual, inscription, or high-register text. | Do not mix ngoko and krama randomly. |
| `tutur-madura` | Short community/everyday Madurese copy. | Ask region and politeness target before public use. | Review required for ceremony or high-politeness contexts. | Account for Sumenep, Bawean, Bangkalan, Pamekasan, Sampang, and Tapal Kuda variation. |
| `tutur-bali` | Everyday greetings and community copy. | Use careful public wording with anggah-ungguh awareness. | Review required for temple, ritual, adat, and status-sensitive text. | Do not improvise sacred formulas. |
| `tutur-sasak` | Everyday Sasak phrases such as greetings, thanks, and etiquette cues when source-backed. | Use short reviewed phrases plus Indonesian support text for public output. | Review required for adat, ritual, and status-sensitive Sasak halus. | Sasak has dialect and social-level variation; ask target dialect when it matters. |
| `tutur-banjar` | Everyday Banjar phrases such as `ulun`, `pian`, `apa habar?`, and `bujur` when source-backed. | Use Banjar-aware Indonesian with clear public facts. | Review required for adat, religious, wedding, and formal community text. | Ask whether the target is Banjar Kuala, Banjar Hulu, or general Banjar. |
| `tutur-bugis` | Everyday Bugis politeness cues such as `tabe'`, `iye`, `idi'`, and `Basa Ugi` when source-backed. | Use Bugis-aware Indonesian with clear public facts. | Review required for adat, religious, wedding, Lontara/script, and formal community text. | Ask target Bugis area/community before public use. |
| `tutur-makassar` | Everyday Makassar cues such as `tabe'`, `iye'`, and `Basa Mangkasara` when source-backed. | Use Makassar-aware Indonesian with clear public facts. | Review required for adat, religious, wedding, Lontara/script, and formal community text. | Distinguish Bahasa Makassar from Makassar Malay or local Indonesian accent. |
| `tutur-batak-toba` | Everyday Batak Toba greetings such as `Horas`, `Mauliate`, and `Hata Batak Toba` when source-backed. | Use Batak Toba-aware Indonesian with clear public facts. | Review required for adat, wedding, marga, church/ritual, kinship, and Batak script text. | Do not guess kinship terms such as `Amang`, `Inang`, `Lae`, `Ito`, `Tulang`, `Hula-hula`, `Boru`, or `Dongan tubu`. |
| `tutur-lampung` | Everyday Lampung phrases such as `Tabik pun`, `Api kabar?`, `Nyow kabar?`, and `Kabar wawai` when source-backed. | Use dialect-labelled Lampung-aware Indonesian with clear public facts. | Review required for adat, ritual, wedding, aksara Lampung, and formal public text. | Do not mix Api/A and Nyo/O dialect forms without labeling. |
| `tutur-betawi` | Requests such as `Bé, beliin Mamat sepédé dong` when the Chaer dictionary covers them. | Keep the errand in Indonesian after the attested request. | Review required for kampung dialect and public print. | Polite lines in that dictionary drop pronouns and use `Bé` or `Bang`. |
| `tutur-palembang` | Everyday lines such as `makan apo kau?` and peer `awak` from the Palembang dictionary. | Keep Bebaso out of family chat. | Review required for keraton Bebaso and upstream Musi. | Do not turn every a into o and call it Palembang. |
| `tutur-rejang` | Short Lebong address lines such as `Mak gen kabar kumu uyo` when that fieldwork gloss is the source. | Longer text stays Indonesian. | Review required for other Rejang dialects and adat. | Do not treat Curup or Kepahiang as the Lebong sample. |
| `tutur-kerinci` | Siulak picture-dictionary words such as `umah`, `lawang`, and `sinanyan`. | Messages stay mixed because the book has words, not sentences. | Review required for other Kerinci kecamatan and adat. | `abang` on the color page means red, not older brother. |
| `tutur-komering` | `tabik` when passing seated elders, plus `nyak` and `niku`, from the Komering dictionary. | Operational detail stays Indonesian. | Review required for adat and unlabeled dialect examples. | `tabik` here is not Lampung `tabik pun`. |
| `tutur-karo` | `mejuah-juah` and `bujur` from the Karo dictionary. | The rest of the message stays Indonesian. | Review required for kinship, adat, and script. | Do not import Toba `Horas` or `Mauliate`. |
| `tutur-simalungun` | `horas` as the dictionary meeting greeting, and short lines such as `ulang ho hanam i jin`. | Words missing from that file stay Indonesian. | Review required for adat and lemmas tagged Tb. | `mauliate` is not in the dictionary file used here. |
| `tutur-mandailing` | `santabi di hamu sude` and `mauliate` from the Angkola-Mandailing dictionary. | Keep Angkola-only labels out of a Mandailing request. | Review required for adat, marga, and kinship. | Do not open a Mandailing chat with Toba-style Horas. |
| `tutur-pakpak` | `njuah-njuah` and `njuah-njuah mo kita karinana` from the Pakpak book. | Name the suak before public use. | Review required for adat and script. | Not Karo `mejuah-juah` and not Horas. `tulanmu` means your bone, not uncle. |
| `tutur-nias` | `Ya'ahowu`, answered with the same word, plus `Ono Niha` and `Li Niha`. | After the greeting, the errand stays Indonesian. | Review required for adat, healing, and wedding formulas. | Do not invent a Nias ceremony around the greeting. |
| `tutur-mentawai` | `abekia` for kabar and `surak` or `sura'` for thanks when the dictionary gloss is the source. | Island and office facts stay Indonesian. Office-wall names stay unglossed. | Review required for uma, sikerei, healing, and wedding. | Do not force one spelling across Siberut, Sipora, and Pagai. |
| `tutur-dayak-ngaju` | Ngaju lines such as `dohop ikei` and `tarima kasih` from the 2013 dictionary. | This folder is Ngaju only. | Review required for ritual and for any other Dayak language. | `ngaju` means upstream. It is not the name of every Dayak language. |
| `tutur-maanyan` | Short glossed lines such as `amahku tulak bagawi` and `lewu yeru haut matueh`. | Ritual pangunraun stays out. | Review required for adat and for Warukin speech that has shifted toward Banjar. | Not Ngaju. Do not paste `yiti` onto an Indonesian sentence. |
| `tutur-kutai` | Tenggarong lines such as `hambat-hambat nyawa blm mendi`. | Kota Bangun is a different catalog entry. | Review required for pantun, Jawi manuscripts, and court text. | Not Banjar Samarinda. `nyawa` is I, `awak` is you. |
| `tutur-paser` | `selamat ma`, `ise kabar iko?`, and `tabe depo ulun tuo`. | Dictionary examples are not labeled by village. | Review required for soyong and adat. | `iko` is you. Do not swap in Kutai `awak` or `mendi`. |
| `tutur-tidung` | Sesayap lines such as `kenjulu tupar seluarku` from the Salimbatu study. | Northern Tidung is a different ISO code. | Review required for other dialects and adat. | Not Kutai and not Banjar. Possession sticks to the noun. |
| `tutur-bajau` | Flores Sea words `ma'i`, `nggai`, `malasso`, and `kampoh` only. | The rest of any message stays Indonesian. | Review required for ceremony and for other Bajau shores. | Not Sabah Bajau, and not one ritual style for every Bajau community. |
| `tutur-toraja` | Visit greeting `manasumo raka?` and short lines such as `puntingku`. | This is Toraja-Sa'dan as documented, not a funeral register. | Review required for Rambu Solo and aluk. | Do not invent funeral or aluk formulas. Belonging sits at the end of the noun. |
| `tutur-mandar` | `meloaq ummande`, `tabeq`, and `diang karewa macoa u tarima`. | Glottal is written `q` in this dictionary. | Review required for adat and for Balanipa, Banggae, and Pamboang. | Not Bugis and not Makassar. Respectful you is `itaq`. |
| `tutur-tolaki` | Open with `tabea` or `tabee`, then the request. | Six Peta Bahasa dialects stay apart. | Review required for adat. | Not Muna and not Wolio. `tabea inggomiu` is for peers, not for talking down to a child. |
| `tutur-muna` | Street greeting `o-k[um]ala ne hamai?` from van den Berg. | Brackets mark an infix. They are not letters to read aloud. | Review required for other Muna dialects and adat. | Not Wolio and not Tolaki. The language name in these lines is `wamba Wuna`. |
| `tutur-wolio` | `tabea`, `tulungi`, `inda pokia`, and `yapai ta porope ingkita?` from the 1985 dictionary. | Words in that dictionary end in a vowel. | Review required for Keraton and sara language in Baadia. | Not Muna. Respectful you is `ingkita`. |
| `tutur-gorontalo` | `Wolo habali?` for kabar and `Odu'olo` for thanks. | Not Manado Malay. | Review required for dialect spelling and for teknonyms. | Do not merge Kota, Suwawa, Atinggola, and Bolango into one spelling. |
| `tutur-minahasa-tombulu` | `Syambae` and `Kura-mo?` as short greetings. | Do not paste Tombulu words onto Melayu Manado. | Review required for dialect and adat. | Not Tonsea, Tontemboan, or Toulour. Phrase-table greetings are support, not a full grammar. |

## Agent Loading Rule

For any skill, load:

1. `SKILL.md`
2. `references/offline-brief.md`
3. `references/local-mirror.md`
4. `references/usage-patterns.md`
5. `references/examples.md`
6. `references/style-guide.md` or `references/lexicon.md` when present

If the target use is public, ritual, legal, educational, or official, keep uncertainty visible and recommend competent speaker review.
